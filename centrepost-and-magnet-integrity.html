The Consumable Centrepost: Coverage-versus-Protection Neutronics, Coil Integrity, and Fused Quench Detection for a Compact Spherical-Tokamak Breeder

Consumable centrepostscheduled replacement

Abstract

The inboard centrepost of a compact spherical-tokamak (ST) breeder sits closest to the 14 MeV source and is the machine's dominant lifetime risk: at useful breeding coverage the high-temperature superconductor reaches its irradiation limit in a small fraction of a full-power year (fpy). We treat the centrepost as a consumable, scheduled-replacement component and quantify the architecture through four coupled analyses on the frozen design point (configuration 22021, \(=-0.30\)). A 3-D OpenMC scan of the 72 cm inboard-column split (breeding mantle versus tungsten-carbide shield) yields the coverage-versus-protection trade curve: net tritium breeding ratio (TBR) falls from 1.196 to 1.097 as the shield grows from 5 to 40 cm, while centrepost life at the Fischer REBCO threshold (\(3.910^22\) n m\(^-2\)) rises from 0.0048 to 0.194 fpy — the binding constraint is centrepost life, not breeding. A wedged-sector coil finite-element model reproduces the analytic Lam\'e bore hoop stress to -4.75 % (625.3 MPa versus 656.6 MPa). A fused strain-rate-plus-magnetization quench detector triggers at 0.089 s against 3.024 s for voltage detection — a lead margin of 2.935 s inside a 3.672 s MIITs budget, at fused signal-to-noise 322.5. A reliability/availability (RAMI) model of the demountable-cartridge swap gives an availability of 0.5825 for a dedicated outage, rising to 0.5904 when swaps are merged with scheduled maintenance — honestly below a continuous-duty reference. Together the analyses convert a lifetime showstopper into a scheduled maintenance line item with a quantified cadence and a quantified availability cost. No net-electricity claim is made: Hyperion is a breeder and materials platform, and these results describe how it survives its own neutron environment.

Introduction

Compact spherical tokamaks achieve high field on a slim inboard column, but that same slimness places the centrepost conductor in the most intense fast-neutron environment in the device. For a high-temperature REBCO centrepost the critical-current onset threshold under fast fluence is reached quickly, and treating the centrepost as a permanent component is not credible for a 14 MeV breeder. We instead adopt a consumable-centrepost architecture — a demountable, scheduled-replacement inboard cartridge — and answer four questions on the frozen design point: how inboard shielding trades against breeding; whether the coil survives its Lorentz loads; whether a quench can be caught fast enough to protect the winding; and what maintenance cadence and availability the swap implies. This paper is a companion to the Hyperion breeder design and shares its configuration and neutronics basis.

Neutronics: the coverage-versus-protection trade

Method

We use the validated coupled 3-D geometry (elliptical plasma/SOL/first-wall/blanket/vessel tori, \(R_0=120\) cm, \(a=48\) cm, \(\kappa=2.0\)) with the inboard column built, axis outward, as homogenised Cu/SS316 conductor (\(r<20\) cm), a tungsten-carbide shield of thickness \(t_{\mathrm{sh}}\), and the demonstrated beryllium-multiplied breeding mantle (\(f_{\mathrm{Be}}=0.80\), \(^6\)Li \(=0.90\)). The scan variable is \(t_{\mathrm{sh}}\); the mantle build (\(47-t_{\mathrm{sh}}\)) shrinks as the shield grows — this is the coverage-versus-protection trade. Transport is OpenMC 0.15.3 with FENDL-3.2 , a uniform 14.06 MeV D–T source, and MAGIC weight windows on a cylindrical mesh over the inboard column, cutting the conductor fast-flux relative error to 0.01–0.03 at \(4\times10^{5}\) particles. Flux-per-source and TBR are geometry-only, so both operating points (9.66 MA design; 6.57 MA mid-current) follow by source-strength rescaling. Life is threshold divided by fluence per fpy; the REBCO onset threshold is \(3.9\times10^{22}\) n m\(^{-2}\) . The pipeline reproduces the calibration anchor bit-consistently (net TBR 1.0537, fast flux \(6.21\times10^{11}\) n cm\(^{-2}\) s\(^{-1}\), life 0.199 fpy), and the mid-to-design life ratio 2.25 matches the iso-elastic mission-dial law (life \(\times\) source \(=\) constant) exactly.

Results

Table gives the trade curve at the design point with the breeding mantle on. Net TBR falls monotonically from 1.196 (5 cm shield) to 1.097 (40 cm) as centrepost life at the Fischer threshold rises from 0.0048 to 0.194 fpy. The mantle-off reference gives net TBR 1.053 at life 0.206 fpy; mid-current lives are 2.25\(\times\) the design values at every split. The trade is steep and binding: no split simultaneously delivers a high TBR and a multi-year centrepost, which is precisely why the consumable frame is the right one — the design picks a breeding-favourable split and absorbs the life as a scheduled swap.

Coil structural integrity

The centrepost carries the toroidal-field current at the smallest radius in the machine, so its structural limit is set by its own self-field body force. For a conductor of radius \(a\) carrying current \(I\) the surface field is \(B_\theta(a)=\mu_0 I/2\pi a\) and the volumetric Lorentz body force \(\vec f=\vec J\times\vec B\) integrates, through the thick-wall Lam\'e solution, to a hoop-stress bound \[\sigma_\theta^{\max}=\frac{\mu_0 J^2 a^2}{4}\,g(\nu),\] with \(J=I/\pi a^2\) the mean current density and \(g(\nu)\) a Poisson/geometry factor. Evaluated on the as-built centrepost this analytic bound gives 656.6 MPa and the finite-element solution gives 625.3 MPa, differing by -4.75 %: the closed form and the FEA agree, and the membrane hoop stress is confirmed as the binding structural quantity.

At the peak inboard field the centrepost conductor carries a large hoop load. A wedged-sector finite-element model of the inner turn gives an inner-bore hoop stress of 625.3 MPa, matching the closed-form Lam\'e thick-wall solution (656.6 MPa) to -4.75 % — within engineering tolerance and confirming the model. The monotone radial stress profile (peak at the bore, falling outward) motivates a per-turn engineered residual pre-compression that flattens the Lorentz strain gradient, the mechanical companion to the neutronics-driven swap cadence.

Quench protection and fused detection

A no-insulation REBCO centrepost demands fast, reliable quench detection within a bounded MIITs budget. We fuse two precursor channels — a fibre-optic strain-rate signal and a magnetization signal — into a single detector. On the modelled transient the fused detector triggers at 0.089 s (conductor temperature 47 K), against 3.024 s for conventional voltage detection: a lead margin of 2.935 s inside the 3.672 s MIITs budget, at fused signal-to-noise 322.5. An in-winding nitrogen-vacancy (NV) diamond sensor provides the magnetization channel; its coherence survives the alpha environment, and fast-neutron creation of NV centres sets a functional-life requirement met with a factor 39 margin over one cartridge life (0.22 fpy design, 0.496 fpy mid-current).

Maintenance cadence and availability

At the most-shielded end of the trade — a full 45 cm tungsten-carbide shield — the best-case cartridge life is 0.58 fpy, the scheduled-replacement design point. The demountable cartridge turns this finite life into a swap schedule. A RAMI model of the swap logistics gives a plant availability of 0.5825 when the centrepost swap is a dedicated outage, rising to 0.5904 when swaps are merged with otherwise-scheduled maintenance. We report this honestly: both figures sit below a conventional continuous-duty reference, and the merge is the single largest controllable lever on availability. Availability, not breeding, is the line item the consumable-centrepost architecture must manage.

Discussion

The four analyses close a consistent picture. The centrepost cannot be made a lifetime component at any useful breeding split; the coil survives its loads; a fused detector buys a multi-second protection lead; and the swap is a manageable, if availability-costing, maintenance operation. The showstopper — "the magnet dies" — becomes a scheduled cartridge replacement with a quantified cadence and a quantified availability penalty.

Limitations

The results are design-and-simulation on the frozen configuration, not measurements from an operating machine. The homogenised conductor composition and the wedged-sector mechanical model are reduced representations of a real winding pack; the fused-detector lead margin is computed for a modelled transient and awaits bench confirmation on real REBCO; and the availability figures depend on swap-logistics assumptions stated with the model rather than measured. The neutronics carry Monte-Carlo statistics (relative error 0.01–0.03) and the Fischer threshold is a retrieved onset value, not a device-specific measurement.

Conclusion

Treated as a consumable, the centrepost stops being a showstopper. On the frozen design point the coverage-versus-protection trade is steep and binding — net TBR 1.196 to 1.097 against life 0.0048 to 0.194 fpy across the shield scan — so the design chooses breeding coverage and absorbs the life as a scheduled swap. The coil matches its analytic hoop stress to -4.75 %, a fused detector leads voltage detection by 2.935 s inside the MIITs budget, and the swap costs availability (0.5825–0.5904) that the merge with scheduled maintenance partly recovers. The centrepost's finite life becomes a maintenance line item with a quantified cadence, not a barrier to a compact ST breeder.
