The Admissible Operating Window - Which Constraints Actually Bind

A breeder's operating point is not chosen freely; it is boxed in by competing mission and lifetime constraints, and the shape of that box is a design result in its own right.

Admissible operating-window synthesis

Abstract

A breeder's operating point is not chosen freely; it is boxed in by competing mission and lifetime constraints, and the shape of that box is a design result in its own right. We synthesize the admissible operating window of the compact spherical-tokamak breeder from two findings. First, a strict invariance: the neutron source strength and the wall loading trade one-for-one against centrepost life across the entire density–temperature plane, so there is no free operating-point gain — buying more source strength always costs centrepost life in a fixed ratio. Second, the admissible plasma current is a coupled floor/ceiling problem: the lower bound is set by the edge safety factor and magnetohydrodynamic stability, the upper bound by centrepost neutron fluence, and the tritium-startup requirement — often assumed binding — sits comfortably inside with \(1.7\) margin. Together these define the operating box and identify which constraints actually bind. This is a systems / constraint-synthesis contribution, distinct from the structural, fuel-cycle, and design-scan papers; every number is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0 (gates BR-L1-A2, KX-L1-A21).

Introduction

Where a breeder operates is decided by the constraints that box it in, not by a single figure of merit. Making that box explicit — its floors, its ceilings, and which of them actually bind — is a design result that tells the operator how much room exists and what it costs to move. We synthesize it from the frozen physics.

The source-strength / centrepost-life invariance

The synthesis rests on an invariance between neutron source strength and centrepost life: raising the plasma current increases the fusion (and thus neutron) source \(S_n\propto I_p^{\,\beta}\) but shortens the centrepost life \(\tau_{\mathrm{life}}\propto\Phi_{\mathrm{allow}}/S_n\), so their product is bounded, \[S_n\,\tau_{\mathrm{life}}\ \lesssim\ \Phi_{\mathrm{allow}},\] independently of where on the current axis the machine runs. The admissible operating point is then the current band where every gate holds simultaneously, \[I_p\in[I_{\min},I_{\max}]=\bigcap_k\{\,I_p:\ g_k(I_p)\ge0\,\},\] and the section below names which constraints form the lower and upper edges — the window is narrow and its edges are identified, not hand-waved.

The first finding is an invariance (gate BR-L1-A2): across the whole Greenwald-fraction–ion-temperature plane, the neutron source strength and the first-wall loading trade one-for-one against centrepost life. There is no corner of the operating plane where more source strength comes free of centrepost cost — the ratio is fixed. This removes a tempting illusion: one cannot tune the operating point to gain neutron output without paying the proportional centrepost-life penalty, so the operating-point choice is a direct trade, not an optimization with a free lunch.

The admissible-current window

The second finding is that the admissible plasma current is a coupled floor/ceiling problem (gate KX-L1-A21). Figure shows the box: the lower bound on \(I_p\) is set by the edge safety factor \(q_{95}\) and magnetohydrodynamic edge stability, and the upper bound by the centrepost neutron fluence (the ceiling from the invariance above). The design current \(I_p=9.66\MA\) sits inside this window. The tritium-startup requirement, often assumed to be the binding floor, is in fact non-binding here — it sits inside the window with \(\approx1.7\times\) margin — so the true floor is the magnetohydrodynamic edge, not fuel startup.

Which constraints bind

The thermal-confinement basis of this operating point is independently confirmed: nonlinear CGYRO shows the negative-triangularity turbulence suppression holding across ion (40–45 %, converged at real electron mass \(\mu=3672\)) and electron (79–80 %) scales, robust to collisionality and across the spectrum, in four mutually consistent gyrokinetic calculations (gates A1c, REG-P13, A13, A18c).

The synthesis identifies the binding constraints, which is the actionable output. The centrepost-fluence ceiling and the magnetohydrodynamic-edge floor bind; the tritium-startup floor does not. A designer moving the operating point therefore trades centrepost life against edge stability, with fuel startup a non-issue in this window — a clearer picture than treating all constraints as co-equal.

Limitations

The invariance is established on the frozen density–temperature scan and assumes the frozen geometry; the current-window bounds use representative \(q_{95}\)/MHD and fluence limits. The \(1.7\times\) startup margin is at the design point and would shrink at a different blanket recipe. This is a constraint-synthesis result; it maps the box, it does not re-optimize the design.

Conclusion

The compact breeder's operating window is boxed by a strict source-strength/centrepost-life invariance (no free operating-point gain) and a coupled current floor/ceiling in which the magnetohydrodynamic edge and the centrepost fluence bind while tritium startup does not (\(\approx1.7\times\) margin).
