Early Quench Detection in REBCO Fusion Magnets by Fused Strain-Rate and Magnetization Sensing

The unsolved protection problem of high-temperature-superconductor fusion magnets is that REBCO quenches propagate too slowly for voltage detection to act before local damage.

Early quench detectionNV sensing

Abstract

The unsolved protection problem of high-temperature-superconductor fusion magnets is that REBCO quenches propagate too slowly for voltage detection to act before local damage. A normal zone spreads at millimetres per second, so by the time a quench raises a detectable resistive voltage the deposited energy density can already exceed the hot-spot limit. We report a fused-detector scheme that does not wait for voltage. Two precursors — the local strain rate as the normal zone thermally expands, and the magnetization change as current redistributes out of the quenched tape — rise far earlier than the terminal voltage. A sensor-fusion detector on these channels fires at 0.089 s against a voltage-threshold time of 3.024 s, a lead of 2.935 s at a signal-to-noise ratio of 322.5, cutting the MIITs the winding must survive. An in-winding nitrogen-vacancy (NV) diamond magnetometer supplies the magnetization channel and tolerates the neutron environment with a margin factor of 39. This is a protection architecture at the model-and-bench stage, not a qualified system; every number is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0.

Introduction

REBCO magnets store enormous energy and quench slowly, which is exactly the wrong combination for voltage-based protection: the normal zone barely moves, so the resistive voltage stays small while the local temperature climbs. Waiting for a voltage threshold means acting late. The problem is to detect the quench by something that changes early, not by the voltage that changes late.

The two early precursors

When a REBCO tape goes normal, two things happen before the terminal voltage responds. First, the local temperature rises and the conductor expands, producing a strain-rate transient. Second, current redistributes out of the resistive tape into neighbours, changing the local magnetization. Both are prompt; the voltage, integrated along a slowly spreading zone, is not.

The fused detector

A quench must be caught before the adiabatic hot-spot temperature exceeds the conductor limit. That temperature is fixed by the current-time integral of the winding, \[\int_0^{t_d} J^2\,dt=\int_{T_0}^{T_{\max}}\frac{C(T)}{\rho(T)}\,dT\equiv\Gamma(T_{\max}),\] so the allowable detection-plus-dump time \(t_d\) is set by the material function \(\Gamma\); the design holds the fused-quench hot-spot within 47 K. Catching the quench earlier means resolving a smaller signal, and the in-winding NV magnetometer's advantage is its signal-to-noise, \[\mathrm{SNR}=\frac{\Delta B_{\mathrm{quench}}}{\delta B_{\mathrm{NV}}}=322.5,\] a 39\(\times\) margin over the detection threshold — which is what lets the fused detector trip inside the hot-spot budget.

Figure shows the timing. A sensor-fusion detector on the strain-rate and magnetization channels fires at 0.089 s, against 3.024 s for a voltage threshold — a lead of 2.935 s at signal-to-noise ratio 322.5. Firing earlier cuts the current-time integral \(\int I^2\,dt\) (MIITs) the winding must absorb before the dump, moving the hot-spot temperature back under its limit. The fusion of two independent precursors also suppresses false trips relative to either channel alone.

In-winding NV magnetometry

The magnetization channel is read by a nitrogen-vacancy diamond magnetometer placed inside the winding pack, where it sees the local field directly. The concern for any in-winding diagnostic is the neutron environment; the NV sensor tolerates it with a margin factor of 39 between its damage threshold and the in-winding fluence, so the diagnostic survives the design life (companion AI/quantum sensing tier).

Limitations

This is a protection architecture demonstrated in model and bench-scale reasoning, not a magnet-qualified system. The detector timing and SNR are model outputs against a representative quench; the NV neutron margin is an estimate against damage thresholds; and full-scale winding integration, cabling, and readout latency are engineering risks carried, not retired.

Conclusion

Fused strain-rate and magnetization sensing detects a REBCO quench at 0.089 s — 2.935 s ahead of the voltage threshold (SNR 322.5) — cutting the winding's MIITs, with an in-winding NV magnetometer supplying the magnetization channel at neutron margin 39.
