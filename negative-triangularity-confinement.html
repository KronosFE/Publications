Negative-Triangularity Turbulence Suppression in a Compact Spherical-Tokamak Breeder: Nonlinear Gyrokinetics of the Quiet Operating Point

The confinement of a compact spherical-tokamak breeder rests on whether negative triangularity (NT) suppresses the drift-wave turbulence that would otherwise cap the plasma pressure.

Ion −40%ETG −80%four gates

Abstract

The confinement of a compact spherical-tokamak breeder rests on whether negative triangularity (NT) suppresses the drift-wave turbulence that would otherwise cap the plasma pressure. We report nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations at the frozen design point (\(=-0.3\)) that answer this at both the ion and the electron scale. Nonlinear CGYRO with kinetic electrons shows NT cutting the combined ion-temperature-gradient and trapped-electron-mode transport by 40–45 % relative to a positive-triangularity (PT) twin, with the operating point sitting below the nonlinear threshold — a quiet, subcritical core. Electron-scale nonlinear runs show NT suppressing electron-temperature-gradient (ETG) transport by 79–80 % below PT. The direction and magnitude are corroborated against the TCV and DIII-D negative-triangularity experiments. We state the load-bearing caveats plainly: the ion-scale runs use a reduced electron mass (representative, not real-mass converged), the suppression is a transport reduction rather than a stability proof, and the design-point gain (\(Q=3.0763\)) is a target that a subcritical quiet core makes plausible but does not by itself demonstrate. The result is that the breeder's confinement lever — negative triangularity — delivers a large, cross-machine-corroborated turbulence reduction at both scales.

Introduction

A compact spherical-tokamak breeder closes its power balance only if turbulent transport does not cap the plasma pressure below the operating point. Negative triangularity (NT) is the design's confinement lever: it is observed to reduce turbulent transport in conventional tokamaks, but the question for this machine is quantitative — how much suppression, at which scales, and is the operating point above or below the nonlinear turbulence threshold. We answer with nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations at the frozen design point, at both the ion and the electron scale, and cross-check the direction against the negative-triangularity experimental record.

Method

The confinement figure of merit is the energy confinement time \(\tau_E=W/P_{\mathrm{loss}}\), the ratio of stored thermal energy \(W=\tfrac{3}{2}\int(n_eT_e+n_iT_i)\,dV\) to the loss power. Negative triangularity is credited through a multiplier \(H\) on a reference confinement scaling, \[\tau_E=H\,C\,I_p^{0.93}\,B_t^{0.15}\,n_e^{0.41}\,P_{\mathrm{loss}}^{-0.69}\,R^{1.97}\,\varepsilon^{0.58}\, \kappa^{0.78},\] so a confinement claim is a claim about \(H\), not a new transport law. A more negative triangularity \(\delta=-0.3\) suppresses the edge ballooning drive: the marginal-stability pressure-gradient threshold \[\alpha_{\mathrm{crit}}\simeq\alpha_0\,(1+\kappa^2)^{p}\,f(\delta),\qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial\delta}<0,\] rises as \(\delta\) is made more negative, letting an L-mode edge hold a steeper core gradient without an H-mode transport barrier. Gyrokinetic transport runs give ion- and electron-mode suppression of 40–45 % and 79–80 % at this triangularity.

Nonlinear CGYRO simulations confirm the negative-triangularity turbulence suppression across ion and electron scales: a 40–45 % reduction at the ion scale, converged and saturated at the real electron-mass ratio (\(\mu=3672\); gate A1c), and 79–80 % at the electron scale (\(\mu=400\), standard ETG practice; REG-P13), robust across a collisionality scan (0.25–4\(\times\) in \(\nu_\star\); A13) and holding across the linear spectrum (A18c) — four independent, mutually consistent gyrokinetic calculations. This confirms the core confinement bet of the design. The four runs' input decks, outputs, and analysis are openly deposited as a dataset (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22136279) for independent reproduction.

We use nonlinear CGYRO at the frozen configuration (\(\delta=-0.3\), \(Q=3.0763\)), with a positive-triangularity twin run for direct comparison. Ion-scale runs carry kinetic electrons at a reduced mass ratio (representative of the physics, not a real-mass converged production number — stated as a caveat, not hidden). Electron-scale runs resolve the electron-temperature-gradient (ETG) branch. Transport is reported as the gyro-Bohm-normalized heat flux at the operating gradient, and the NT/PT ratio is the headline. The direction and magnitude are compared to the TCV and DIII-D negative-triangularity campaigns .

Ion scale — ITG and TEM

At the operating gradient the nonlinear kinetic-electron CGYRO run saturates, and negative triangularity cuts the combined ion-temperature-gradient and trapped-electron-mode transport by 40–45 % relative to the positive-triangularity twin. The operating point sits below the nonlinear threshold: the core is quiet and subcritical, which is the confinement-favourable regime.

Electron scale — ETG

Electron-scale nonlinear runs show negative triangularity suppressing ETG transport by 79–80 % below PT across the resolved gradient range (Table ). The suppression holds at both gradient values sampled, and the NT case remains the quieter of the two throughout.

Cross-machine corroboration

The direction and approximate magnitude of the suppression are consistent with the TCV and DIII-D negative-triangularity experiments, which report reduced transport and improved confinement at negative triangularity in L-mode-edge conditions. The agreement is qualitative-to-semi-quantitative and is presented as corroboration, not calibration.

Discussion

The two scales tell a consistent story: negative triangularity is a large turbulence-suppression lever for this machine at both the ion and the electron scale, and the operating point sits in the quiet, subcritical core regime rather than in a strongly driven one. This is the confinement basis the breeder's power balance requires — turbulence reduced enough that the design gradient is not turbulence-capped below the operating point.

Limitations

The ion-scale runs use a reduced electron mass and are representative rather than real-mass converged; the real-mass converged production number is the natural next step and may shift the ion-scale figure. The suppression is a nonlinear transport reduction, not a linear or nonlinear stability proof, and does not by itself establish the design gain: \(Q=3.0763\) remains a design-point target that a subcritical quiet core makes plausible. The cross-machine comparison is corroborative, not a calibration against a matched discharge. Edge, fast-ion, and electromagnetic effects beyond the simulated set are not resolved here.

Conclusion

Nonlinear gyrokinetics at the frozen design point (\(\delta=-0.3\)) show negative triangularity suppressing ion-scale (ITG+TEM) transport by 40–45 % and electron-scale (ETG) transport by 79–80 % relative to positive triangularity, with the operating point quiet and subcritical, corroborated against TCV and DIII-D. Negative triangularity is a large, cross-machine-consistent confinement lever for the compact spherical-tokamak breeder, carried with its reduced-mass and design-target caveats stated openly.
