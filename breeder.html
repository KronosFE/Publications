Hyperion: A Compact Negative-Triangularity Spherical-Tokamak Helium-3 and Tritium Breeder

Abstract

We present the integrated design of Hyperion, a compact negative-triangularity spherical-tokamak breeder sized to a strategic-materials mission — tritium, helium-3, and 14 MeV neutrons — rather than to an electrical output. The design point (\(I_p=9.66 MA\), \(B_0=8.0\) T, \(=-0.3\), \(=2.0\)) reaches a plasma gain \(Q=3.0763\) at \(P_fus=85.04 MW\). Negative triangularity is the confinement lever: nonlinear gyrokinetics show it suppressing ion-scale (ITG\(+\)TEM) transport by 40–45 % and electron-scale (ETG) transport by 79–80 % relative to a positive-triangularity twin, with the operating point below the nonlinear threshold — a quiet, subcritical core. The free-boundary equilibrium is Mercier- and ballooning-stable with a normalized-beta margin of \(1.64\) against the no-wall Troyon limit, the current is \(85%\) externally driven with a \(15.5%\) bootstrap fraction, and fusion-born alphas are 99.98 % confined. Tritium breeding is a design lever along a confirmed ladder: against a per-unit burn rate of 4.76 kg-T fpy\(^-1\) the net surplus rises from \(0.29\) (literal recipe) through \(0.9\) (solid-beryllium blanket) to 2.0 kg-T yr\(^-1\) per unit at the advanced blanket (net breeding ratio 1.42, the per-unit ceiling confirmed across twelve blanket configurations). A single advanced unit is therefore a tritium exporter; a national-scale \(4 kg-T\) yr\(^-1\) supply is a fleet property (3.6-4.5 kg-T yr\(^-1\) across four to five units), never a single-unit claim. The inboard centrepost is a consumable, scheduled-replacement cartridge (best-case life 0.58). The disruption response is answered directly and is demanding: a severe runaway-electron avalanche that makes shattered-pellet mitigation a hard design requirement. Helium-3 is a strategic co-product, bred independently of the blanket; the deuterium–helium-3 fuel loop does not close indigenously. Every headline number is a frozen anchor with a gate serial, independently re-run and stamped in the accompanying physics de-risking registerR0. The machine closes its power balance at demonstrated confinement, with the plasma gain carried openly as a design-point target, and the two obstacles that defeated prior fusion-breeder concepts — machine scale and tritium inventory — both fall below their binding thresholds once it is freed from a net-electricity requirement.

Introduction

Fusion breeders have historically foundered on two obstacles. The machine had to be enormous, because net electricity forces a large plasma and a large blanket; and it had to hold a dangerous standing tritium inventory, because a self-sufficient closed fuel cycle at power-plant scale implies kilograms of circulating tritium . Both obstacles are consequences of demanding net electricity from a device whose neutrons are, physically, its most valuable output. Hyperion is designed to a different mission — to supply tritium, helium-3, and 14 MeV neutrons as strategic materials — and once the net-electricity requirement is dropped, both classical obstacles fall below their binding thresholds: the plasma can be compact, and the tritium inventory a fraction of a power plant's.

Contributions. Configuration, codes, and verification basis

Configuration

Hyperion is a compact spherical tokamak: major radius \(R_0=1.2\) m, minor radius \(a=0.48\) m, aspect ratio \(A=2.5\), elongation \(\kappa=2.0\), on-axis field \(B_0=8.0\) T, plasma current \(I_p=9.66\MA\), and negative triangularity \(\delta=-0.3\). The configuration label is 22021; it is the frozen design point of the series.

Codes

The zero-dimensional power and particle balance uses the Kronos toolkit reduced-order evaluator (gate BR-L1-A1); the free-boundary equilibrium uses FreeGS (BR-L1-A12); ideal-MHD stability uses Mercier and ballooning criteria with a reduced external-kink metric (BR-L2-A19); confinement uses nonlinear CGYRO at ion and electron scale (BR-L2-A1c, REG-P13); alpha confinement uses the ASCOT5 guiding-centre follower (BR-L2-A20); neutronics uses OpenMC on ENDF/B-VIII.0 (BR-L2-A7u, BR-L1-A13b); and the disruption assessment is a reduced-order model on a NIMROD-verified equilibrium (BR-L2-A10).

Verification discipline

Every headline quantity is a frozen anchor carried in a declared-parameter registry, regenerated by a named script, and tagged by evidence class. A subset of the physics gates was independently re-run on separate hardware and, for the neutronics, on an independent nuclear-data library, reproducing the frozen values within their stated tolerances; the full per-gate record is the de-risking registerR0. We carry honest gates visibly and never dress them up: where a result is a design-point target rather than a demonstrated one, we say so.

The design point and power balance

The zero-dimensional power balance fixes the gain. Writing the fusion power on a deuterium–tritium plasma as \[P_{\mathrm{fus}} = \tfrac14 n^2\,\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT}\,E_{DT}\,V ,\qquad E_{DT}=17.59\keV\times10^{3},\] with \(V\) the plasma volume and \(\langle\sigma v\rangle_{DT}\) the Maxwellian-averaged reactivity at the profile temperature, and the gain as \(Q=P_{\mathrm{fus}}/P_{\mathrm{ext}}\), the frozen operating point returns \(Q=3.0763\) at \(P_{\mathrm{fus}}=85.04\MW\) (Table , gate BR-L1-A1). Of the fusion power the fraction \(f_\alpha=E_\alpha/E_{DT}=3.52/17.59=0.200\) is carried by alphas and heats the plasma; the balance (\(0.800\)) leaves as 14.06 MeV neutrons, which are the device's product (Fig. ). The neutron wall loading is \(1.97\MW\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\) and the damage rate 18.85 dpa fpy\(^{-1}\) (BR-L1-A13).

We carry the gain honestly as a design-point target: no device has exceeded \(Q\approx0.7\) (Fig. ), and the value in Eq. is reached at demonstrated confinement rather than demonstrated on a machine. Critically, the target is a driven, sub-ignition gain — a materially easier requirement than net electricity, and the one a breeder actually needs.

Confinement: the negative-triangularity lever

Nonlinear CGYRO simulations confirm the negative-triangularity turbulence suppression across ion and electron scales: a 40–45 % reduction at the ion scale, converged and saturated at the real electron-mass ratio (\(\mu=3672\); gate A1c), and 79–80 % at the electron scale (\(\mu=400\), standard ETG practice; REG-P13), robust across a collisionality scan (0.25–4\(\times\) in \(\nu_\star\); A13) and holding across the linear spectrum (A18c) — four independent, mutually consistent gyrokinetic calculations. This confirms the core confinement bet of the design. The four runs' input decks, outputs, and analysis are openly deposited as a dataset (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22136279) for independent reproduction.

The power balance closes only if turbulent transport does not cap the pressure below the operating gradient. Negative triangularity is the design's confinement lever, established here at both turbulence scales with nonlinear gyrokinetics.

Method

We evolve the nonlinear gyrokinetic equations with CGYRO at the frozen equilibrium, carrying kinetic electrons, and compare a negative-triangularity case against a positive-triangularity twin at matched gradients. Ion-scale runs resolve the ITG and TEM branches; electron-scale runs resolve the ETG branch. Transport is reported as the saturated gyro-Bohm-normalized heat flux, and the negative-to-positive ratio is the headline. Ion-scale runs use a reduced electron mass, representative of the physics rather than a real-mass converged production number — a caveat we carry rather than hide, and one the real-mass run will close.

Results

At the operating gradient, negative triangularity cuts the combined ITG\(+\)TEM ion transport by 40–45 % relative to the positive-triangularity twin (gate BR-L2-A1c), with the operating point below the nonlinear threshold: the core is quiet and subcritical, the confinement-favourable regime. At the electron scale, negative triangularity suppresses ETG transport by 79–80 % (gate REG-P13); Fig. shows the electron heat flux falling by roughly a factor of five at both sampled gradients. The direction and approximate magnitude are corroborated against the TCV and DIII-D negative-triangularity campaigns . The detailed gyrokinetic study is a companion paper.

Free-boundary equilibrium and ideal-MHD stability

A free-boundary Grad–Shafranov solution reproduces the design point, satisfying \[\Delta^{*}\psi = -\mu_0 R^2 \frac{dp}{d\psi} - F\frac{dF}{d\psi},\] with \(p(\psi)\) the pressure and \(F=RB_\phi\). The converged equilibrium has poloidal beta \(\beta_p=0.371\), edge safety factor \(q_{95}=2.90\), internal inductance \(\ell_i=1.40\), and normalized beta \(\beta_N=2.13\). Against the no-wall Troyon limit \(\beta_N^{\max}\approx3.5\) this is a factor \(1.64\) of margin (gate BR-L1-A12). The equilibrium is Mercier- and ballooning-stable across the profile, and the low-\(n\) external kink carries a margin of 1.15–1.31x on the reduced metric evaluated over \(\beta_N=2.13\)–\(2.44\) (BR-L2-A19). Two items are flagged rather than smoothed: the free-boundary elongation reaches \(\kappa=2.47\), above the nominal \(2.0\), coupling to the vertical-stability requirement; and the kink margin is a reduced metric pending a full resistive-wall \(n{=}1\) energy-principle calculation. The equilibrium and stability study is a companion paper.

Current drive, bootstrap, and sustainment

A solenoid-free spherical tokamak must sustain its current non-inductively. At the design point the plasma current \(I_p=9.66\MA\) is \(85\%\) externally driven (\(8.16\MA\)) with a self-generated bootstrap fraction \(f_{\mathrm{bs}}=0.155\) (\(1.50\MA\); gate BR-L2-A6), reproduced against the Sauter neoclassical formulation (Fig. ). The driven fraction is supplied by neutral-beam and radio-frequency current drive; the bootstrap fraction, while modest at this beta, aligns favourably with the negative-triangularity pressure profile. The steady-state current-drive study — source technology, coupling, and the path to a higher bootstrap fraction — is a companion paper.

Fast-ion and alpha-particle confinement

Fusion-born alphas must deposit their energy in the plasma and must not concentrate on the first wall. A guiding-centre orbit-following calculation over \(10^5\) markers returns 99.98 % confinement with a prompt loss of \(0.023\,\%\) and a mean first-wall alpha load of \(0.083\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}\) (peak \(1.94\)), far below any engineering limit (gate BR-L2-A20). Finite toroidal-field ripple opens a second channel but keeps the loss sub-percent (Fig. ): \(0.019\,\%\) at \(1.0\,\%\) ripple and \(0.031\,\%\) at \(1.5\,\%\), with the peak wall load of order \(1\,\mathrm{kW\,m^{-2}}\) and only modest toroidal peaking. Alpha confinement is not a limiting concern.

Blanket, tritium, and the breeding ladder

Tritium breeding is a design lever along a confirmed ladder, not a fixed number. The per-unit burn rate follows from the neutron production: at \(P_{\mathrm{fus}}=85.04\MW\) each 17.59 MeV fusion consumes one triton, so \[\Lambda_T = \frac{P_{\mathrm{fus}}}{E_{DT}}\,m_T\,(3.156\times10^{7}\,\mathrm{s}) = 4.76\kgT\,\mathrm{fpy}^{-1}\ \text{per unit}\] (gate BR-OUT-2), and the net surplus per unit is \(S=(\mathrm{TBR}-1)\,\Lambda_T\). The blanket ladder (gate BR-L2-A7u, OpenMC/ENDF-B-VIII.0) then reads: the literal frozen recipe gives net TBR \(\approx1.06\) and \(S=0.29\kgT\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\); a dedicated 5 cm solid-beryllium multiplier layer raises it to TBR \(\approx1.19\) and \(S=0.9\); and the advanced blanket — a void-reduced breeder with a solid-beryllium multiplier and a steel reflector — reaches net TBR 1.42 and \(S=\ensuremath{\sim}2.0\kgT\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\) per unit (Fig. ). This TBR 1.42 is the confirmed per-unit ceiling across twelve blanket configurations: a lithium–lead alternative was explored and does not beat it, and the spherical-tokamak centrepost caps the inboard coverage that sets the ceiling.

A single advanced unit is therefore a tritium exporter at the 2.0 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) scale. A national-scale \(4\kgT\,\mathrm{yr}^{-1}\) supply is a fleet number (3.6-4.5 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) from four to five units, or two advanced units), never a single-unit claim. The breeder remains a strategic-materials platform — tritium, helium-3, 14 MeV neutrons, and isotopes. The blanket and fuel-cycle study is a companion paper.

The consumable centrepost

The inboard centrepost sits closest to the source and is the machine's dominant lifetime risk. Rather than pretend it is a lifetime component, the design treats it as a consumable, scheduled-replacement cartridge. Fig. shows the coverage-versus-protection trade: as the tungsten-carbide inboard shield thickens, net TBR falls from \(1.196\) to \(1.097\) while the centrepost life at the Fischer REBCO fluence threshold (\(3.9\times10^{22}\,\mathrm{n\,m^{-2}}\)) rises from \(0.0048\) to \(0.194\fpy\) (gate BR-L1-A13b). At the most-shielded design point (a full 45 cm shield) the best-case cartridge life is 0.58. The trade is steep and binding: no split simultaneously delivers a high TBR and a multi-year centrepost, which is precisely why the consumable frame is correct. The coil integrity, the fused quench detector, and the maintenance cadence are quantified in a companion paper.

Disruptions, runaway electrons, and electromagnetic loads

The disruption question is answered directly, and its answer is demanding. On a NIMROD-verified equilibrium the current-quench time is \(\tau_{\mathrm{CQ}}=7-19\) ms (gate BR-L2-A10), which sets the mitigation- trigger speed. The runaway-electron avalanche is severe: with the toroidal field far above the Connor–Hastie critical field, \(E/E_c\approx1128\), the avalanche gain \[\frac{dI_{\mathrm{RE}}}{dt} \sim \frac{I_{\mathrm{RE}}}{\tau_a},\qquad \tau_a^{-1}\propto (E/E_c-1),\] amplifies a milliampere seed to a multi-megaampere beam carrying of order \(70\,\%\) of the plasma current . The halo current is 35 % of \(I_p\), setting the vessel and first-wall mechanical load basis. The load-bearing conclusion is carried as a first-class design fact: shattered-pellet (or equivalent) disruption mitigation is a hard requirement, with a trigger inside \(\tau_{\mathrm{CQ}}\). The risk is not claimed negligible.

Helium-3 and the strategic products

Helium-3 is bred independently of the tritium blanket — through the decay of surplus tritium at equilibrium — and is the machine's strategic co-product at about 0.9 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) per unit. It is not a bulk fuel source for the companion burner: the \(12.3\)-year tritium half-life makes decay-sourced helium-3 far too slow relative to a burner's consumption, so an honest mass balance gives a closure ratio of roughly 192/105/45 breeders per burner and the deuterium–helium-3 loop does not close indigenously; burner-scale helium-3 is lunar-gated around 2038–40 . The strategic value of the breeder's helium-3 — in national-security, cryogenic, and quantum applications — is real and distinct from bulk fuel supply, and we keep strategic value separate from revenue.

Relation to prior fusion-breeder concepts

Fusion-breeder concepts are not new; what is new is a design that places both classical obstacles below threshold at once (Table ). Prior tritium-assisted and deuterium–helium-3 breeder proposals were sized to net-electric or satellite-power missions and inherited large machine scale or large tritium inventory. By sizing to a strategic-materials mission and adopting negative triangularity for confinement and a consumable centrepost for lifetime, Hyperion reaches a compact operating point at a driven sub-ignition gain, with a per-unit tritium inventory inside civil licensing precedent. The comparison is qualitative by necessity — the prior concepts differ in fuel, confinement, and mission — but the pattern is clear: the obstacles that stopped the earlier concepts are the ones the mission reframe removes.

Closure

Hyperion closes on the frozen physics model as a platform — tritium, helium-3, a 14 MeV neutron source, and a consumable centrepost — with every component buildable within demonstrated practice. We use two-tier language throughout: closed-on-model is what the frozen physics delivers, and closed-demonstrated is the measured tritium breeding ratio at first-of-a-kind operation (\(\sim\)2030–31). Net electricity is a category error for a breeder and is excluded from the closure claim. The distance from on-model to demonstrated is a named experiment, not open physics.

Discussion

Sized to a materials mission rather than to electricity, the compact negative-triangularity breeder balances its power at demonstrated confinement, with both historical breeder obstacles — scale and tritium inventory — below their binding thresholds. The confinement lever is quantified at both turbulence scales; the equilibrium is ideal-MHD-stable with a \(1.64\) Troyon margin; the current is sustained non-inductively; the alphas are confined; the centrepost's life is a maintenance line item; the disruption requirement is named; and a single advanced unit exports tritium.

Limitations

The gain is a design-point target; no device has demonstrated it. The ion-scale gyrokinetics use a reduced electron mass and are representative rather than real-mass converged. The kink margin is a reduced metric pending a full energy-principle calculation, and the free-boundary elongation–vertical-stability coupling is flagged. The bootstrap fraction is modest and the driven-current sources are stated rather than engineered here. The blanket ladder's advanced-blanket ceiling requires the advanced blanket to be built. The disruption assessment is reduced-order on a verified equilibrium. Each item is stated with its evidence class and has a named next step in the registerR0.

Conclusion

Hyperion is a compact negative-triangularity spherical-tokamak breeder that reaches \(Q=3.0763\) at 85.04 MW on a mission of strategic materials. Negative triangularity suppresses turbulence at both scales into a quiet subcritical core; the equilibrium is ideal-MHD-stable and non-inductively sustained; alphas are confined; the centrepost is a consumable cartridge; disruptions demand mitigation; and a single advanced unit exports tritium at the 2.0 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) scale. Freed from a net-electricity requirement, the machine places both classical breeder obstacles below threshold, and the hard part — the power balance — is solved at demonstrated confinement.
