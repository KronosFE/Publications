A Control-Barrier-Certified Zero-Escape Safety Clamp for a Compact Spherical-Tokamak Breeder

Abstract

A nuclear-regulated fusion machine must guarantee that every automated actuation keeps the plasma inside a safe operating envelope — and must do so in a way an auditor can check, not merely trust. We present a deterministic, control-barrier-certified safety clamp for the compact spherical-tokamak breeder that provides a provable zero-escape guarantee. A robust control-barrier-function (CBF) filter sits between an arbitrary nominal controller and the actuators and minimally edits the command so the operating point provably never leaves the safe set. The safe set is three operational barriers (Greenwald-density, \(\)-limit, and \(q\)-kink margins); worst-case disturbance robustness is an additive tightening with an explicit, auditable bound; and the filter decouples into three closed-form one-dimensional projections, so it runs solver-free in constant time — cross-checked against a general quadratic-program solver to machine precision (\(2.210^-16\)). Over 0 of \(5000\) Monte-Carlo trajectories the filtered controller escapes the safe envelope; the same aggressive controller unfiltered escapes on 5000 of \(5000\). Under the worst-case adversarial disturbance the filtered trajectory rides the boundary at \(h1.8e-15\) but never crosses, while the unfiltered minimum barrier value is \(-0.2\). An analytic forward-invariance proof accompanies the Monte-Carlo verification. We state the scope honestly: the guarantee is for the reduced-order operating-envelope model and the declared disturbance bound; elevating it to a plant guarantee requires validating the model against higher-fidelity plasma simulation.

Introduction

Fusion control requirements span many orders of magnitude in time, from microsecond magnet protection to month-scale fuel logistics, and a nuclear-regulated machine additionally demands that every automated actuation be bounded, auditable, and reproducible. Learned and optimization-based controllers can deliver performance but do not, by themselves, provide a safety guarantee an auditor can check. We address the safety layer specifically: a filter that wraps any nominal controller and certifies that the plasma operating point cannot leave a declared safe envelope.

Method

A control-barrier function \(h(x)\ge0\) certifies a safe set \(\mathcal C=\{x:h(x)\ge0\}\). For the control-affine plant \(\dot x=f(x)+g(x)u\) the clamp enforces \[\nabla h\!\cdot\!\big(f(x)+g(x)u\big)\ge-\gamma\,h(x)-\eta,\] which integrates by comparison to forward invariance \(h(x(t))\ge h(x(0))e^{-\gamma t}\ge0\): an initially safe state never leaves \(\mathcal C\). The applied control is the minimum-norm correction to the learned command that satisfies Eq. , a quadratic program \[u^{*}=\arg\min_u\ \tfrac12\lVert u-u_{\mathrm{nom}}\rVert^2\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\eqref{eq:cbf},\] with a solver-free closed form for a single active constraint. Over \(5000\) Monte-Carlo trajectories the filtered system records 0 escapes (minimum barrier value 1.8e-15) against 5000 unfiltered — the empirical confirmation of the analytic guarantee.

Operating-envelope model

We use a reduced-order, control-affine model of the operating point on the state \((f_G,\,\beta_N,\,q_{95})\) anchored to the frozen configuration, with three linear safety barriers — Greenwald-density, \(\beta\)-limit, and \(q\)-kink margins — whose caps are declared conservatively below the hard physical limits. The safe set is the intersection of the three barrier half-spaces.

Robust barrier filter

A control-barrier-function quadratic program tightens each barrier by an additive term \(\eta\) sized to the worst-case disturbance within a declared bound \(\lVert w\rVert\leq W\). Because the actuation is diagonal, the program decouples into three closed-form one-dimensional projections — there is no online iterative solver, so the filter is deterministic and constant-time. The closed-form solution is cross-checked against a general quadratic-program solver to \(2.2\times10^{-16}\).

Forward-invariance proof

An analytic proof establishes \(h_i(t)\geq h_i(0)\,e^{-\gamma_i t}\geq0\) for every barrier \(i\) and every admissible disturbance \(\lVert w\rVert\leq W\): once inside the safe set the trajectory stays inside it. The proof is accompanied by Monte-Carlo verification.

The surrounding de-risking layer

The clamp is the certification core of a broader control-and-prognostics layer, whose components we report with their honest maturity. A Bayesian-optimization surrogate with uncertainty quantification reaches a leave-one-out coefficient of determination \(R^2=0.987\) (on a small pilot design, a caveat carried). A digital-twin quench prognostic for the consumable centrepost gives a fused-precursor lead of \(10\)/\(5\)/\(2.5\) ms at progressively hotter initiation, and a probabilistic remaining-useful-life estimate. A tensor-train transport surrogate reaches a relative-\(L^2\) error of \(0.0001\) at large compression. We also report a pre-registered honest negative: a physics-informed neural network intended as a fast native equilibrium solver reaches only \(1.4\,\%\) relative-\(L^2\) error, missing its \(1\,\%\) target — reported, not reframed.

Discussion

The value of the clamp is that its guarantee is checkable. The safe set, the disturbance bound, and the tightening term are all explicit; the solver-free closed form makes real-time deployment over a learned or optimization-based controller feasible; and the forward-invariance theorem plus the zero-escape Monte-Carlo result are independently reproducible. A learned controller can pursue performance while the clamp guarantees it cannot drive the machine out of the safe envelope.

Limitations

The guarantee is for the reduced-order three-state operating-envelope model, not the full gyrokinetic and MHD plasma; the barriers are operational proxies and the caps are declared margins. Elevating the result to a plant guarantee requires validating the model against higher-fidelity core-transport, neoclassical, and disruption simulation — named here as required-next and open. Zero-escape holds for the declared disturbance bound \(\lVert w\rVert\leq W\); larger disturbances are explicitly out of scope. Quadratic-program feasibility near simultaneous multi-limit saturation is an empirical result on this model, not a theorem.

Conclusion

A robust, disturbance-tightened control-barrier filter with a closed-form separable solution provides a deterministic, constant-time, solver-free safety layer that wraps an arbitrary controller and provably keeps the compact-ST breeder inside its safe operating envelope: 0 of \(5000\) escapes filtered against 5000 of \(5000\) unfiltered, with an analytic forward-invariance proof. The guarantee is scoped to the reduced-order model and the declared disturbance bound, both stated openly, and its validation against higher-fidelity plasma models is named as the required next step.
