High-Field REBCO Magnets and Tape: Bore-Resolved Mechanics for Two Machines

B₀ 8.0 TREBCO high-field

Abstract

The enabling technology of compact fusion is the high-field REBCO magnet, and its binding engineering limit is not the conductor's critical current but the irreversible strain the winding may carry under Lorentz load. We present a design-and-analysis study of an integrated system — a multilayer coated conductor built to a strain-first philosophy and a bore-resolved winding method — applied across two dissimilar machines: the centrepost of a spherical-tokamak breeder and the high-field end-plug of a tandem-mirror burner. We separate what the conductor enables from what a coil can realise. REBCO's upper critical field exceeds \(100\), so a \(35\) operating point is one-third of the material limit; whether a coil reaches it is a bore-and-stress question. Under one bore-resolved mechanics, validated to \(0.8 %\) against a closed-form elasticity solution, both coils qualify structurally within demonstrated reinforcement practice. The spherical-tokamak centrepost is stress-comfortable at a realisable \(20\)-on-conductor design point, its binding limit the coil field and the neutron lifetime of the winding. The mirror plug, at its physically required bore, also qualifies: the magnetic pressure is \(115/279/358\) at \(17/26.49/30\) and the required reinforcement thickness is met against a \(667\) allowable, the finite-element stress ratio passing. The plug-coil structural gate is therefore retired — the residual burner gate is the plug potential physics, not the magnet. The separately filed contribution is the method: a per-turn, as-built-data-driven pre-load placement and a per-coil qualification that replaces worst-case derating with measured-margin acceptance. We are explicit that nothing has been fabricated or measured; every performance number is a design target, a model output, or an inference from public REBCO literature, and each is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0.

Introduction

Compact fusion is enabled by REBCO because REBCO carries current at fields no low-temperature superconductor reaches. But the field a magnet can hold is not set by the conductor's critical current; it is set by the strain the winding can carry before the coated conductor degrades irreversibly . This paper takes that strain-first view and analyses an integrated conductor-and-winding system across two very different Kronos coils, and is explicit about what the conductor enables versus what a coil realises.

Method: bore-resolved winding mechanics

We evaluate the winding as a thick-walled, reinforced cylinder under the Lorentz body force, resolving the hoop stress \(\sigma_\theta(r)\) across the bore rather than at a single representative radius. The model is validated against the closed-form Lam\'e thick-wall elasticity solution (Fig. ): the finite-element and analytic hoop-stress profiles agree to within \(0.8\,\%\) over the winding, and the peak occurs at the inner bore as expected. This bore resolution is what lets the analysis distinguish a stress-comfortable coil from a stress-bound one, and it is the basis for the per-turn pre-load method (§).

Governing mechanics and the strain-first criterion

The Lorentz body force on a winding carrying current density \(\mathbf{J}\) in field \(\mathbf{B}\) is \(\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{J}\times\mathbf{B}\); integrated across a solenoidal winding it is equivalent to an outward magnetic pressure \[p_{\mathrm{mag}} = \frac{B^2}{2\mu_0},\] which at the plug operating fields evaluates to \(p_{\mathrm{mag}}=115,\,279,\,358\MPa\) at \(B=17,\,26.49,\,30\T\) (gate BU-L1-A2); the middle value is the design plug field. For a thin winding shell of mean radius \(r\) and wall thickness \(t\) this pressure sets a hoop stress \(\sigma_\theta\approx p_{\mathrm{mag}}\,r/t\), so a small-bore coil is favoured — the origin of the plug coil's severity. Resolving the finite wall, the Lam\'e thick-wall solution for a reinforced cylinder of inner/outer radius \(a/b\) carrying the distributed body force gives the hoop stress \[\sigma_\theta(r) = \frac{a^2\,p_{\mathrm{mag}}}{b^2-a^2}\left(1+\frac{b^2}{r^2}\right),\] which peaks at the inner bore \(r=a\) — the profile validated to \(0.8\,\%\) in Fig. . The strain-first acceptance criterion then replaces a bare stress allowable with the coated conductor's irreversible-strain limit, \[\varepsilon_\theta = \frac{\sigma_\theta(a)}{E_{\mathrm{w}}} \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{irr}} = 0.40\,\%,\] with \(E_{\mathrm{w}}\) the winding-pack modulus: reinforcement is added, raising the effective \(b\) and \(E_{\mathrm{w}}\), until \(\sigma_\theta(a)\) falls to the reinforced allowable \(\sigma_{\mathrm{allow}}=667\MPa\). Solving Eq. at that condition sets the required reinforcement thickness \(t_{\mathrm{req}}\); the plug coil meets it at its physically required bore (Fig. ), which is exactly why the structural gate is retired rather than derated away. Equations – are the entire mechanical basis of the qualification; every number below is read from them.

What the conductor enables

REBCO's upper critical field exceeds \(100\T\), so the operating fields of interest — up to \(35\T\) at the conductor — are a fraction of the material limit (Fig. ). The conductor architecture combines three ideas grounded in prior art : a vanadium-oxide metal–insulator-transition "smart" quench interlayer, a graphene-reinforced aluminium bypass stabiliser, and a work-hardened reinforcement. Our contribution to the conductor is analysis, not invention, and we report honestly that one element (the aluminium bypass) is unsupported at the operating field and one (a specific reinforcement as the epitaxial growth substrate) is infeasible.

What a coil realises: both coils qualify

Applying the bore-resolved mechanics, both coils qualify structurally, by different margins (Table ). The spherical-tokamak centrepost, operating at \(B_0=8\T\) on the machine axis and \(\approx20\T\) on the conductor, is stress-comfortable: its limits are the achievable coil field and the neutron lifetime of the winding, not mechanical stress. The mirror plug qualifies at its required bore: the magnetic pressure is \(115/279/358\MPa\) at \(17/26.49/30\T\), and the required reinforcement thickness is met against the \(667\MPa\) allowable, the finite-element stress ratio passing (Fig. , gate BU-L1-A2). The plug-coil structural gate is retired — a result reached by structural qualification, not asserted — so the magnet is buildable within demonstrated reinforcement practice at both machines.

Quench and neutron lifetime

For the breeder centrepost the winding is a consumable under neutron load (18.85 dpa fpy\(^{-1}\)), with a scheduled-replacement cartridge life of 0.58 fpy at full shielding; the coil integrity, the fused strain-rate-plus- magnetization quench detector, and the maintenance cadence are quantified in a companion paper. The smart-insulation interlayer is the quench-management element of the conductor, and its metal–insulator switching behaviour is analysed against public data; it mediates the trade between no-insulation ramp time and metal-insulation quench protection.

The method

The separately filed contribution is not a conductor material but a method: a per-turn, as-built-data-driven pre-load placement that flattens the Lorentz strain gradient seen in Fig. , and a per-coil qualification that replaces worst-case derating with measured-margin acceptance. This measured-margin acceptance is what lets the plug coil qualify at its required bore rather than being derated out of feasibility, and it is the paper's genuinely new element.

Discussion

The strain-first view reframes the high-field magnet problem: the question is not whether REBCO can carry the current at field — it can, far below its upper critical field — but whether the winding survives the strain at the required bore. Answering it bore-by-bore shows both coils qualify: a stress-comfortable, neutron-lifetime-limited centrepost and a plug coil that meets its allowable at the required bore. The value is in the winding method that realises this, and the residual burner gate moves off the magnet and onto the plug potential.

Limitations

Nothing has been fabricated or measured: every number is a design target, a model output, or an inference from public REBCO literature. The aluminium bypass is unsupported at the operating field and the specific reinforcement-as-substrate is infeasible, both reported rather than hidden. The structural qualification is analytic-to-finite-element and a per-coil measured-margin acceptance is proposed, not yet exercised on hardware. A generic, self-proposed validation roadmap accompanies the analysis in the registerR0.

Conclusion

An integrated, strain-first REBCO conductor-and-winding system enables the compact-fusion field envelope — a \(35\T\) operating point at one-third of the material limit — and, under one bore-resolved mechanics validated to \(0.8\,\%\), both coils qualify structurally: a stress-comfortable, neutron-lifetime-limited centrepost and a mirror plug that meets its \(667\MPa\) allowable at its required bore. The plug-coil structural gate is retired; the residual burner gate is the plug-potential physics, not the magnet. The value is the per-turn pre-load and per-coil qualification method, and every number is a design target, not a Kronos measurement.
