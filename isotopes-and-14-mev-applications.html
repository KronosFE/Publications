Isotope Co-Production and 14\,MeV Materials Qualification at a Spherical-Tokamak Breeder

A breeder is a strong 14 MeV neutron source before it is anything else, and that source has value beyond tritium.

Isotopes & 14 MeV applications

Abstract

A breeder is a strong 14 MeV neutron source before it is anything else, and that source has value beyond tritium. We describe two co-products of the compact spherical-tokamak breeder. First, a high-flux fusion-spectrum irradiation volume for materials qualification: the design delivers a full-rate fast-neutron capability about \(59\) the reference dedicated materials facility, in a true fusion spectrum rather than an accelerator surrogate — a capability the fusion materials program has lacked. Second, medical and industrial isotopes bred parasitically in dedicated target stations, including the molybdenum-99 / technetium-99m supply chain that periodically faces global shortage, produced without a fission reactor and its proliferation-sensitive targets. These are co-products of the neutron economy, booked as strategic materials rather than revenue, and consistent with the no-economics firewall of the public record. This is a capability projection from the frozen source, not an operating facility; every number is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0.

Introduction

The neutron a breeder makes to breed tritium is a resource with more than one use. The same 14 MeV source that drives the blanket can qualify materials in a true fusion spectrum and breed medical isotopes in target stations. This paper scopes both co-products from the frozen source strength.

A 14 MeV materials-qualification facility

A fusion neutron source is quantified for applications by the reaction rate it drives in a sample. The production of a target isotope obeys \[\frac{dN}{dt}=N_t\,\sigma\,\phi-\lambda N\ \Rightarrow\ N(t)=\frac{N_t\sigma\phi}{\lambda}\big(1-e^{-\lambda t}\big),\] with \(N_t\) target atoms, \(\sigma\) the production cross-section, \(\phi\) the neutron flux, and \(\lambda\) the product decay constant; the saturation activity \(A_{\mathrm{sat}}=N_t\sigma\phi\) is set purely by the source flux. The same flux drives displacement damage at a \(14\,\mathrm{MeV}\)-representative rate, so one source serves both the isotope-production and the materials-qualification missions.

The fusion materials program has never had a high-flux, fusion-spectrum irradiation source; accelerator-based sources (IFMIF-class) approximate the spectrum at limited volume. Figure places the breeder's irradiation capability at about \(59\times\) the reference dedicated materials facility in full-rate fast-neutron capability, in a genuine fusion spectrum. This turns the breeder into a materials-qualification platform for the whole field, not only for its own components (companion materials paper).

Medical and industrial isotopes

Dedicated target stations in the neutron field breed isotopes parasitically. The molybdenum-99 / technetium-99m diagnostic supply chain — subject to recurrent global shortage from an ageing fission-reactor fleet — can be produced at a fusion source without fissile targets and their proliferation and waste burdens. Other neutron-activation isotopes for industry and research follow from the same target infrastructure. These are strategic-material co-products, booked as capability, not revenue (no-economics firewall).

Limitations

Both co-products are capability projections from the frozen source strength and target-station design, not operating yields; specific isotope production rates depend on target design, chemistry, and extraction not detailed here. The materials-facility multiplier compares modelled full-rate capability to reference facility specifications, not measured throughput.

Conclusion

The breeder's 14 MeV source is a co-production platform: a fusion-spectrum materials-qualification capability about \(59\times\) the reference facility, and a fission-free route to shortage-prone medical isotopes such as molybdenum-99.
