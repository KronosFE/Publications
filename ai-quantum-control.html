Physics-Informed AI and Quantum Technologies for Certifiable Real-Time Control of Compact Fusion Generators

AIMLquantum — clamped, certifiable

Abstract

Fusion power plants impose control requirements spanning ten orders of magnitude in time — microsecond magnet protection through month-scale fuel logistics — and, for a nuclear-regulated machine, demand that every automated actuation be bounded, auditable, and reproducible. We present a layered digital-twin control architecture and hold its three technology-readiness tiers strictly apart. Physics-informed machine learning is deployable now, in the control loop, when its error is bounded and deterministically clamped: a control-barrier safety filter catches all safe-set violations with zero escapes over \(5000\) Monte-Carlo trajectories against \(5000\) escapes unfiltered, backed by an analytic forward-invariance proof; a Bayesian-optimization surrogate reaches a leave-one-out coefficient of determination of \(0.987\); and a tensor-train transport surrogate reaches a relative-\(L^2\) error of \(1.010^-4\) at large compression. Quantum sensing is a near-term diagnostics-tier technology that sharpens the state estimate. Quantum computation is, for fusion, an offline calibration tier with no resource crossover this decade: Landau damping reproduces on a five-qubit circuit to \(1\)–\(7 %\) and variational quantum chemistry reaches chemical accuracy on the alloy fragments, but the linear kinetic operator needs \(11\)–\(19\) logical qubits and the nonlinear gyrokinetic problem is fault-tolerant-horizon. We report every missed pre-registered target — the physics-informed equilibrium network did not become a fast native solver, reaching \(1.40 %\) against a \(1 %\) goal — rather than reframing it. The contribution is a system and a method, certifiable and resource-honest, not new plasma physics; each result is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0.

Introduction

A nuclear-regulated fusion generator must be controlled across ten orders of magnitude in time, and every automated actuation must be bounded, auditable, and reproducible. Machine learning and quantum technologies are often invoked for this without a clear statement of what is deployable now versus what is a decade away. We present a layered control architecture and hold three technology-readiness tiers strictly apart, reporting each with its evidence and its misses.

Tier 1: physics-informed AI, deployable now if clamped

The load-bearing requirement is that a learned or optimization-based controller cannot drive the machine out of its safe operating envelope. A robust control-barrier-function (CBF) filter provides this. For a control-affine system \(\dot x=f(x)+g(x)u\) and a safe set \(\mathcal C=\{x:h(x)\ge0\}\), the filter enforces \[\dot h(x,u) \ge -\gamma\,h(x)-\eta,\qquad \eta = \text{worst-case-disturbance tightening},\] which guarantees forward invariance \(h(x(t))\ge h(x(0))e^{-\gamma t}\ge0\): once inside the safe set the state cannot leave it. The clamp is realized as a quadratic program that changes the learned nominal control \(u_{\mathrm{nom}}\) by the least amount consistent with the barrier constraint, \[u^{*}=\arg\min_{u}\ \tfrac12\lVert u-u_{\mathrm{nom}}\rVert^{2} \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \nabla h\!\cdot\!\big(f(x)+g(x)u\big)\ge-\gamma\,h(x)-\eta ,\] which for a single active constraint has the solver-free closed form \(u^{*}=u_{\mathrm{nom}}+\max\!\big(0,\,-(\nabla h\!\cdot\!(f+gu_{\mathrm{nom}})+\gamma h-\eta)\big)\, (g^{\top}\nabla h)/\lVert g^{\top}\nabla h\rVert^{2}\), so the filter runs in bounded time inside the control loop; by Nagumo's theorem the enforced constraint renders \(\mathcal C\) forward-invariant. Over \(5000\) Monte-Carlo trajectories, 0 escape the safe set with the filter against 5000 of \(5000\) without it (Fig. , gate A13), backed by an analytic forward-invariance proof and a solver-free closed form. Around it, a Bayesian-optimization surrogate reaches leave-one-out \(R^2=0.987\) and a tensor-train transport surrogate reaches relative-\(L^2\) 0.0001 at large compression. Physics-informed AI is deployable in the loop because its error is bounded and deterministically clamped; the safety-clamp study is a companion paper.

Tier 2: quantum sensing, near-term diagnostics

Quantum sensing — magnetometry and interferometry at or near the quantum limit — is a near-term diagnostics-tier technology that sharpens the plasma and magnet state estimate feeding the control loop (Fig. ). It is an enhancement of the sensing layer, not a replacement for it, and its readiness is nearer than quantum computation's; the in-winding nitrogen-vacancy magnetometer for quench precursors is one concrete near-term use (companion magnet paper).

Tier 3: quantum computation, an offline calibration tier

For fusion, quantum computation is an offline calibration tier with no resource crossover this decade. Landau damping reproduces on a 5-qubit Hamiltonian-simulation circuit to 1–7 %, and variational quantum chemistry reaches chemical accuracy (better than 1.6 mHa) on the fusion-alloy fragments. But the honest resource split is load-bearing (Fig. ): the linear kinetic operator needs 11–19 logical qubits (early fault-tolerant), and the nonlinear gyrokinetic problem that would most help the machine is fault-tolerant-horizon. Noisy hardware today shows no advantage; the classical baseline is cheaper at every demonstrated scale. The quantum study is a companion paper.

Reporting the misses

We report every missed pre-registered target rather than reframing it. The physics-informed equilibrium network was intended to become a fast native solver and did not: it reached 1.4 % relative-\(L^2\) error against a \(1\,\%\) goal, and physics-informed variants performed far worse. Keeping this negative in the record is part of the method.

Discussion

The value of the architecture is that its tiers are honestly separated and its safety layer is certifiable. A learned controller can pursue performance while a proven clamp guarantees the machine stays in the safe envelope; quantum sensing improves the state estimate near-term; and quantum computation is placed where it actually is — an offline calibration tier for this decade. The contribution is a system and a method, not new plasma physics.

Limitations

The certified guarantee is for the reduced-order operating-envelope model and a declared disturbance bound; elevating it to a plant guarantee requires validation against higher-fidelity plasma simulation. The surrogates are trained on limited pilot data, a caveat carried. The quantum results are simulator-scale demonstrations and resource estimates, not hardware advantage. The equilibrium-network miss is reported, not reframed.

Conclusion

A layered digital-twin control architecture holds three technology-readiness tiers apart: physics-informed AI is deployable now when clamped (0/\(5000\) safe-set escapes with a proven filter); quantum sensing is near-term diagnostics; and quantum computation is an offline calibration tier with no crossover this decade. Every miss is reported, including the equilibrium network's 1.4 % against a \(1\,\%\) target. The contribution is a certifiable, resource-honest system and method for compact-fusion control.
