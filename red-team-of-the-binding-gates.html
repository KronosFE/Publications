Red-Teaming the Honest Negatives: Bounded Closing Paths for a Compact Spherical-Tokamak Breeder and a Deuterium-Helium-3 Tandem-Mirror Burner

Red-team of the binding gates

Abstract

An honest design carries its negatives openly; a useful design then asks whether each negative can be closed. We red-team the binding gates of a compact spherical-tokamak breeder and a deuterium–helium-3 tandem-mirror burner with a disciplined reduced-order method: for each honest negative, brainstorm alternatives, bound each cheaply, rank them, and promote only the promising ones to high-fidelity validation — keeping ruled-out alternatives as reported negatives. For the breeder, three gates narrow. The current-drive power burden, which exceeded the fusion power, drops from 118 to 50 MW (\(-58 %\), below fusion power) at a source gain of \(1.68\) by moving to an operating point inside unused \(\) headroom; tritium closure, fragile against the beryllium-multiplier fraction, is restored to an upper bound of \(1.20\) by a multiplier-independent margin stack; and a reduced-standoff test port reaches 19.7 dpa fpy\(^-1\) without changing mission power. For the burner, the plug-density requirement — the entry gate \(n_p/n_c=16.0\) — is attacked: a hot-electron thermal barrier lowers the required ratio to \(3.5\), and a centrifugal rotating-mirror architecture removes it entirely (\(1.0\)) at the cost of a new rotational-stability question. Every number here is a reduced-order screening bound that states direction and magnitude, not a banked result: the value is to tell high-fidelity computation where to spend its time, and to show that the closing levers were hiding in unused design headroom rather than requiring new physics.

Introduction

A design program earns trust by stating its negatives without dressing them up. But a stated negative is only the start: the next question is whether it is a hard limit or a bounded, closable gap. This paper applies a single disciplined method to the binding gates of both Kronos machines. For each honest negative we brainstorm three to five alternatives, bound each with a cheap reduced-order or analytic model, rank them, and promote only the winners to high-fidelity validation. Ruled-out alternatives are reported as negatives, not hidden. The output is a set of screening bounds: statements of the direction and magnitude of each closing lever, explicitly not banked results.

The breeder: three gates

On the confinement side the core negative-triangularity bet is now retired as a risk rather than assumed: nonlinear CGYRO confirms the turbulence suppression across ion (40–45 %, converged at real electron mass \(\mu=3672\)) and electron (79–80 %) scales, robust to collisionality and across the spectrum, in four independent gyrokinetic calculations (gates A1c, REG-P13, A13, A18c).

A red-team search asks whether any point in the design space clears every gate at once. Feasibility is the non-emptiness of the intersection of the gate constraints, \[\mathcal F=\bigcap_k\{\,\vec p:\ g_k(\vec p)\ge0\,\},\] and the binding margin at a candidate point \(\vec p\) is its worst single constraint, \[m(\vec p)=\min_k g_k(\vec p),\] so a design is only as strong as its weakest gate. Searching \(m(\vec p)\) adversarially finds that three gates set the breeder margin and the plug-density gate binds the burner — the honest failure surface, located rather than avoided.

Current-drive power burden

The current-drive power needed to sustain the plasma exceeded the fusion power in the baseline, with a small bootstrap fraction. Moving to an advanced operating point — higher temperature, larger bootstrap fraction, and a modest fast-ion beam, all inside unused \(\beta\) headroom — drops the current-drive power from 118 to 50 MW (\(-58\,\%\)), now below the fusion power, while raising the neutron source by \(1.68\times\); the operating point uses \(49\,\%\) of the \(\beta\) ceiling. The lever was unused pressure headroom, not new physics.

Tritium closure fragility

Tritium closure in the baseline hung on a beryllium-multiplier fraction more than twice the demonstrated value, with a thin margin. A multiplier-independent margin stack — outboard-blanket thickness, a sacrificial inboard liner, lithium-6 enrichment, and a reflector — restores closure to an upper bound of \(1.20\) even at a realistic multiplier fraction. The stack is sub-additive, so the \(1.20\) is an upper bound, stated as such.

Materials-testing rate

The machine is a high-fluence volume source rather than a high-rate point source. A reduced-standoff re-entrant test port places specimens where the flux is highest, reaching 19.7 dpa fpy\(^{-1}\) on a conservative geometric bound without changing mission power. The competing lever — raising plasma current — does reach a higher rate but trades centrepost life, and is reported as a conditional negative rather than promoted.

The burner: the plug-density gate

The burner's binding gate is the plug-density requirement: the frozen relation \(n_p/n_c=\exp(e\phi_i/T_e)\) reproduces the design point exactly at \(n_p/n_c=16.0\), a plug potential \(165.8\times\) the demonstrated tandem-mirror record. Two independent levers shrink the requirement. A hot-electron thermal barrier raises the carrier temperature that holds the potential — acting inside the exponent — and lowers the required ratio to \(\approx3.5\). A centrifugal rotating-mirror architecture supplies axial confinement by rotation rather than by an electrostatic plug, removing the density-ratio gate entirely (\(\approx1.0\)) at the cost of a new gate — reactor-scale supersonic rotation and its interchange stability — which is where high-fidelity work must go next. Weaker or net-negative alternatives (a higher-field plug coil, for one) are reported as ruled out.

Discussion

Across both machines the closing levers were hiding in unused design headroom — the pressure budget, the blanket radial envelope, the specimen standoff, the carrier temperature — rather than in new physics. That is the useful finding of a red-team: the honest negatives narrow from "accepted limitation" to "bounded, closable with a named path." Each promoted path is a candidate for high-fidelity validation, and only after that validation is a number banked.

Limitations

Every headline here is a reduced-order screening bound that states direction and magnitude; none is a banked result. The breeder current-drive point awaits a self-consistent transport-and-current-drive calculation; the tritium margin stack awaits three-dimensional neutronics; the plug alternatives await kinetic and stability validation. The centrifugal architecture removes the density gate but introduces a rotational-stability question this analysis does not resolve. The burner's neutron-source performance is robust and independent of the plug gate, but the plug potential requirement is attacked here, not retired.

Conclusion

Red-teaming the honest negatives narrows every binding gate to a bounded, named closing path. The breeder's current-drive burden falls \(58\,\%\) below fusion power inside unused \(\beta\) headroom; tritium closure is restored to a \(1.20\) upper bound independent of the multiplier fraction; a reduced-standoff port reaches 19.7 dpa fpy\(^{-1}\); and the burner plug requirement falls from \(n_p/n_c=16.0\) toward \(\approx3.5\) with a thermal barrier or \(\approx1.0\) with a rotating mirror. These are screening bounds that direct high-fidelity validation, not results — but they show the negatives are closable, and where.
