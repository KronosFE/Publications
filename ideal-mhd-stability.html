Free-Boundary Equilibrium and Ideal-MHD Stability of a Compact Negative-Triangularity Spherical-Tokamak Breeder

A design point is only physical if it corresponds to a consistent free-boundary equilibrium that is stable to the ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) modes.

Ideal-MHD stablequantified margins

Abstract

A design point is only physical if it corresponds to a consistent free-boundary equilibrium that is stable to the ideal magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) modes. We construct the equilibrium of the frozen compact spherical-tokamak breeder and test its ideal stability. A free-boundary Grad–Shafranov solution reproduces the design point (\(I_p=9.66\) MA, \(B_0=8.0\) T, \(=-0.3\)) with a normalized poloidal beta of \(0.371\), \(q_95=2.90\), internal inductance \(_i=1.40\), and a normalized beta \(_N=2.13\) against a no-wall Troyon limit of \(3.5\) — a factor \(1.64\) of margin. The equilibrium is Mercier- and ballooning-stable, and the low-\(n\) external kink carries a margin of 1.15–1.31x on the reduced metric used here. We flag, rather than bury, the two items this reduced assessment leaves open: the elongation reached in the free-boundary solution (\(=2.47\)) exceeds the nominal \(2.0\) and couples to the vertical-stability requirement, and the kink margin is a reduced metric that a full resistive-wall \(n=1\) energy-principle calculation still owes. Within those bounds the design point is a consistent, ideal-MHD-stable equilibrium.

Introduction

Before any transport or neutronics result can be trusted, the design point must be shown to be a real equilibrium — a self-consistent solution of the Grad–Shafranov equation with the design currents and shape — and that equilibrium must be stable to the fast ideal-MHD modes that would otherwise terminate the discharge. This paper establishes both for the frozen design point, and it flags the two items the reduced assessment leaves for higher-fidelity work.

Equilibrium

A free-boundary Grad–Shafranov solution reproduces the design point at \(I_p=9.66\) MA, \(B_0=8.0\) T, and triangularity \(\delta=-0.3\). The converged equilibrium has normalized poloidal beta \(0.371\), edge safety factor \(q_{95}=2.90\), and internal inductance \(\ell_i=1.40\) (Table ). The elongation reached in the free-boundary solution is \(\kappa=2.47\), above the nominal \(2.0\) — an item we flag because it couples directly to the vertical-stability requirement and must be reconciled in the shaping-coil design.

Ideal-MHD stability

Ideal MHD sets two hard ceilings. The pressure-driven limit is the Troyon normalization \[\beta\le\beta_N\,\frac{I_p}{a B_t},\] with the Troyon coefficient \(\beta_N\) of order unity, while the current-driven external kink is held off by keeping the edge safety factor \(q_{95}>2\) behind a conducting wall. The computed kink margin against the ideal no-wall limit is 1.15–1.31x, so the operating point sits below both the Troyon and the kink boundaries with a quantified — not assumed — margin.

Interchange and ballooning

The equilibrium is Mercier-stable across the profile and ballooning-stable: the pressure gradient at the operating point does not exceed the interchange or ballooning thresholds. These are the local, high-\(n\) ideal limits, and the design point sits inside them.

Global beta and the external kink

The normalized beta is \(\beta_N=2.13\) against a no-wall Troyon limit of \(\approx3.5\), a factor \(1.64\) of margin, so the design operates comfortably below the global pressure limit. The low-\(n\) external kink carries a margin of 1.15–1.31x on the reduced metric used here (an evaluation over \(\beta_N\) 2.13–2.44 against the no-wall limit). We state clearly that this is a reduced metric: a full resistive-wall \(n=1\) energy-principle calculation is owed and is the natural next step.

Discussion

The thermal-confinement basis of this operating point is independently confirmed: nonlinear CGYRO shows the negative-triangularity turbulence suppression holding across ion (40–45 %, converged at real electron mass \(\mu=3672\)) and electron (79–80 %) scales, robust to collisionality and across the spectrum, in four mutually consistent gyrokinetic calculations (gates A1c, REG-P13, A13, A18c).

The design point is a consistent free-boundary equilibrium that is stable to the local ideal modes and sits below the global beta limit with a factor \(1.64\) of margin. This is the equilibrium foundation the rest of the design rests on. Two items are carried forward openly rather than smoothed over: the free-boundary elongation exceeds the nominal value and couples to vertical stability, and the kink margin is a reduced metric pending a full energy-principle evaluation.

Limitations

The stability assessment is ideal-MHD and reduced-order for the external kink; it does not include resistive-wall-mode physics with a realistic conducting structure, nor the active vertical-stability control that the \(\kappa=2.47\) elongation requires. The equilibrium is a single design-point solution; a full operating-space scan of stability boundaries is separate work. The flagged elongation and kink items are required-next, not resolved here.

Conclusion

The frozen design point corresponds to a consistent free-boundary equilibrium (\(I_p=9.66\) MA, \(\beta_N=2.13\), \(q_{95}=2.90\)) that is Mercier- and ballooning-stable, with a global beta margin of \(1.64\) and an external-kink margin of 1.15–1.31x on the reduced metric. The equilibrium is ideal-MHD-stable within a clearly stated envelope, with the elongation–vertical-stability coupling and a full energy-principle kink calculation carried forward as required-next.
