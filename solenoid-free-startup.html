Solenoid-Free Startup and Non-Inductive Ramp-Up of a Compact Spherical-Tokamak Breeder

A spherical tokamak has almost no room on the inboard side for a central solenoid, so it cannot start its plasma current the way a conventional tokamak does.

Solenoid-free startup

Abstract

A spherical tokamak has almost no room on the inboard side for a central solenoid, so it cannot start its plasma current the way a conventional tokamak does. This is a defining constraint, not a detail: the machine must initiate and ramp its full current without an inductive transformer. We describe the startup and ramp-up strategy for the compact breeder, initiating the current by a combination of solenoid-free techniques — outboard poloidal-field induction, electron-cyclotron pre-ionization and heating, and helicity or beam-driven seed current — and handing off to the non-inductive drive and bootstrap current that sustain the flat-top described in the current-drive companion. The ramp is the transient bridge from a seed current to the \(9.66 MA\) steady state; its cost is the ramp-phase auxiliary power and its risk is the current-profile control during the hand-off. We state the strategy and flag the ramp as an engineering risk carried rather than a solved sequence. This is a design-strategy paper referencing the current-drive companion for the sustained state; every number is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0. This is a shorter synthesis paper.

Introduction

The central solenoid a conventional tokamak uses to induce and ramp its plasma current does not fit in a spherical tokamak's slender centrepost. Startup is therefore not a solved preliminary but a defining physics and engineering problem: the current must be born and grown without a transformer.

Initiating the current without a solenoid

A spherical tokamak has no room for a central solenoid, so the current must be initiated without inductive volt-seconds from the core. The flux state is governed by helicity balance, \[\frac{dK}{dt}=2\,V_{\mathrm{inj}}\Psi-\frac{2}{\tau_K}K,\qquad K=\int\vec A\cdot\vec B\,dV,\] where \(V_{\mathrm{inj}}\) is the injector voltage, \(\Psi\) the injected flux, and \(\tau_K\) the helicity-decay time: sustained injection drives current when the drive term exceeds resistive decay. The subsequent inductive ramp must then close the volt-second balance \[\int V_{\mathrm{loop}}\,dt=L_p I_p+\int R_p I_p\,dt,\] so the hand-off succeeds only if the non-solenoidal start delivers enough current for the outer poloidal-field coils to take over the flux swing.

The strategy combines established solenoid-free techniques. Outboard poloidal-field coils provide a limited inductive flux swing; electron-cyclotron pre-ionization and heating break down the gas and form the initial plasma; and a seed current is established by helicity injection or beam drive. None of these alone reaches the operating current, but together they produce a controllable seed from which the current can be grown.

The ramp and the hand-off

The ramp is the transient that grows the seed current to the \(9.66\MA\) flat-top, at which point the non-inductive drive and the \(1.50\MA\) bootstrap current sustain the state (companion current-drive paper). The load-bearing risks are the auxiliary power required during the ramp phase and the control of the current profile through the hand-off, where the plasma transitions from externally grown to self-consistently sustained. We book both as engineering risks carried into the design, not as a solved sequence.

Limitations

This is a design-strategy paper; the startup and ramp are described as a strategy and a risk register, not demonstrated as a controlled sequence in the frozen model. The seed-current technique, the ramp auxiliary power, and the profile-control scheme are subject to change as the operating scenario is developed. The sustained flat-top is the subject of the current-drive companion.

Conclusion

The compact breeder starts and ramps its current without a central solenoid, using outboard induction, electron-cyclotron heating, and a helicity or beam seed, then hands off to the non-inductive and bootstrap currents that hold the \(9.66\MA\) flat-top.
