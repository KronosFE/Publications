Synchrotron Radiation Transport - What Pins the Burner Temperature

Synchrotron radiation transporta^⅓ scaling

Abstract

Unlike a deuterium–tritium tokamak, a hot deuterium–helium-3 mirror is radiation-dominated: at the electron temperatures required for advanced-fuel reactivity the plasma loses more power to electron-cyclotron (synchrotron) emission than to any other channel, and that loss is what pins the optimal electron temperature. We report a first-principles electron-cyclotron radiation-transport calculation for the burner central cell and show that the synchrotron loss is neither the optically-thin estimate nor a fixed fraction of fusion power. At the design point the radiated power is \(2546 MW\); the reabsorbed synchrotron loss is \(1803 MW\) by a relativistic Maxwell–J\"uttner harmonic-emission transport calculation, against \(919 MW\) by the Trubnikov formula and \(66,677 MW\) optically-thin — a discrepancy of nearly two orders of magnitude that a design cannot leave unresolved. Booking the wall-reflection factor and the finite-mirror harmonic cutoff, the plasma is optically thick to harmonic \(n^*10.4\), and the synchrotron loss scales with machine size through a cutoff that grows as the one-third power of minor radius. This transport calculation collapses a previously \(37\) uncertainty band, sets the maximum-\(Q_E\) electron temperature, and is the physics behind the burner's size-scaling. Introduction

The case against advanced fuels has always centred on radiation: at the high electron temperatures a deuterium–helium-3 plasma needs, cyclotron and bremsstrahlung losses can overwhelm the fusion power. Whether the machine closes therefore depends on getting the synchrotron loss right, not on an order-of-magnitude estimate. This paper computes it from first principles and shows what it implies for the operating point.

The model spread is the problem

Figure shows why the transport calculation matters. Against a radiated-power scale of \(2546\MW\), the synchrotron loss ranges from \(919\MW\) (Trubnikov) to \(1803\MW\) (relativistic Maxwell–J\"uttner harmonic transport with wall reflection) to \(66{,}677\MW\) optically-thin — the last unphysical because the plasma is optically thick at the low harmonics that carry the power. A design that adopted any single estimate without the transport calculation would be off by up to \(37\times\) in its dominant loss channel (gate KX-L1-A3-BU).

Optical depth, harmonic cutoff, and size scaling

Synchrotron transport in the plug is a reabsorption problem: the low harmonics are optically thick and only the high-harmonic tail escapes. The optical depth of harmonic \(n\) across a plasma of size \(a\) is \[\tau_n\simeq\alpha_n(T_e,\theta)\,\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega_{ce}\,c}\,a,\] so the escaping power is the emission integrated only above the cutoff harmonic \(n^{*}\) where \(\tau_{n^{*}}\sim1\), \[n^{*}\propto\Big(\frac{\omega_{pe}^2}{\omega_{ce}}\,a\Big)^{1/3}\sim a^{1/3}.\] This gives the characteristically weak \(a^{1/3}\) growth of the radiated loss with machine size. Because different codes truncate the harmonic sum and the wall-reflection coefficient differently, the model spread — not the physics — is what the transport study pins down, fixing the maximum-\(Q_E\) temperature below.

The plasma is optically thick to an effective harmonic \(n^{*}\approx10.4\); above it the emission escapes. Because the harmonic cutoff where the Rayleigh–Jeans surface meets the optically-thin bound grows with machine size — as the one-third power of the minor radius — the synchrotron loss fraction falls as the machine grows. This is the analytic origin of the burner's size-scaling: a longer central cell amortizes the radiation burden, which is why net electric output climbs with device length (companion burner paper).

The maximum-\(Q_E\) temperature

Because synchrotron loss rises steeply with electron temperature while fusion power saturates, there is an optimal \(T_e\) that maximizes the engineering gain. The transport calculation locates it and fixes the design point at \(Q_E=1.318\) with a neutron fraction of 5.44 %. The temperature is not a free knob; it is set by the radiation balance computed here.

Limitations

The transport calculation assumes a wall-reflection coefficient and a representative density/temperature profile; the reflection factor and profile shape are carried as inputs. The finite-mirror correction is an analytic bracket, not a full three-dimensional emission calculation. The result sets the operating temperature; it does not change the plug-gated closure of the machine.

Conclusion

The deuterium–helium-3 mirror is radiation-dominated, and a first-principles electron-cyclotron transport calculation is required to place its operating point: synchrotron loss is \(1803\MW\) (reabsorbed) against a \(2546\MW\) radiated scale, collapsing a \(37\times\) model band, with the plasma optically thick to \(n^{*}\approx10.4\) and a loss fraction that falls with machine size.
