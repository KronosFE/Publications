Aegis and MetroVolt: A Deuterium-Helium-3 Tandem-Mirror Generator

We present the integrated design of a deuterium–helium-3 tandem-mirror generator sized for low-neutron power production.

Q_E 1.318f_n 5.44%+850 MWe @ 440 m

Abstract

We present the integrated design of a deuterium–helium-3 tandem-mirror generator sized for low-neutron power production. At the design point the plant energy multiplication is \(Q_E=1.318\) at fusion power 4298.5 MW, with a neutron fraction of only 5.44 % of the total power — the low-activation advantage that motivates the fuel choice. A length ladder gives net electric output of +104/+850/+2832 at \(55/440/1400\) m device length, and an uncertainty quantification over the design ranges returns a probability \(P(Q_E>1)=1.0\), driven principally by the central-cell beta. The binding physics gate is the end-plug potential: confining the central-cell ions requires a plug density ratio \(n_p/n_c=16\), a requirement-class target more than two orders of magnitude beyond the demonstrated tandem-mirror record, retired only by a dedicated plug-potential experiment. The plug's loss-cone microstability sits exactly on the drift-cyclotron-loss-cone stabilization knee at a 6.25 % warm-ion fill, a threshold rather than a margin. Direct energy conversion is a bounded upside lever, not the closure mechanism: we take its module efficiency as a load-bearing input (\(_dec=0.7\)) and show the recirculating plug power gates \(Q_E\). The high-field plug coil qualifies structurally within demonstrated reinforcement practice, so the coil structural gate is retired and one residual physics gate remains. We are explicit about what is robust — the neutron-source performance — and what is conditional: every net-power number is contingent on retiring the plug-potential gate.

Introduction

A deuterium–helium-3 mirror is attractive because it produces few neutrons, and it is difficult because it needs a large electrostatic plug to confine its ions and a fuel that is scarce on Earth. This paper is the integrated generator design: the power balance, the length scaling, the binding plug-potential gate, the plug microstability, the role of direct energy conversion, and the structural status of the high-field plug coil. We separate throughout what is robust from what is conditional on the plug gate.

The design point and power balance

At the design point the generator reaches \(Q_E=1.318\) at fusion power 4298.5 MW, with a neutron power of 233.95 MW — a neutron fraction \(f_n=5.44\,\%\) of the total (Table , gate BU-L1-A1). The low neutron fraction is the advantage of the fuel: the primary deuterium–helium-3 reaction \(\mathrm{D}+{}^3\mathrm{He}\rightarrow p\,(14.7\,\mathrm{MeV})+\alpha\) is aneutronic, and the residual \(f_n\) arises only from the deuterium–deuterium side reactions; it minimizes activation and neutron damage relative to a deuterium–tritium machine of the same power. The plant energy multiplication is gated not by the fusion gain but by the power recirculated to sustain the plug and thermal barrier: \[Q_E = \frac{\eta_{\mathrm{th}}\,(P_{\mathrm{fus}}+P_{\mathrm{aux}}) + \eta_{\mathrm{dec}}\,P_{\mathrm{ch}}} {P_{\mathrm{plug}}+P_{\mathrm{aux}}} ,\] so that \(Q_E\) improves with recovered charged power \(P_{\mathrm{ch}}\) but is bounded from below by the recirculating plug power \(P_{\mathrm{plug}}\).

The binding gate: the plug potential

The generator's binding physics gate is the end-plug potential. Confining the central-cell ions against axial loss requires a plug electrostatic potential \(\phi_i\), and the plug density needed to build it follows the Boltzmann relation \[\frac{n_p}{n_c} = \exp\!\left(\frac{e\phi_i}{T_e}\right),\] so that at \(\phi_i=248.75\keV\) and carrier temperature \(T_e=89.7\keV\) the required ratio is \(\exp(248.748/89.717)=16\) exactly (gate BU-L2-A5). Because the requirement sits inside the exponent, the carrier temperature is the strongest lever on it (Fig. ). This ratio is a requirement-class target: \(\phi_i\) is more than two orders of magnitude beyond the demonstrated tandem-mirror plug-potential record, and it is retired only by a dedicated plug-potential experiment at reactor-relevant scale, not by design study. We state this plainly and do not claim it retired.

Plug microstability

The plug's loss-cone distribution is a free-energy source for the drift-cyclotron-loss-cone instability. Its marginal stability sits exactly on the knee: a warm-ion fill of 6.25 % is the \(\gamma=0\) stabilization threshold, not a comfortable margin, and it clears the instability only for sufficiently broad plug-edge density gradients (gate BU-L2-A5). The detailed microstability study — including a weak residual Alfv\'en-ion-cyclotron mode — is a companion paper.

Synchrotron reabsorption and the length scaling

The generator's principal new result is that synchrotron loss is not a fixed fraction of fusion power but scales with machine size, and this is what makes the length ladder work. Electron-cyclotron emission at harmonic \(n\) has an optical depth \(\tau_n\) that decreases with \(n\); the plasma is optically thick and re-emits as a black body up to a cutoff harmonic \(n^{*}\) where \(\tau_{n^{*}}\sim1\), while the harmonics above \(n^{*}\) escape. The reabsorbed loss is therefore set by the cutoff, \[P_{\mathrm{sync}} \simeq \int_{n^{*}}^{\infty}\!\eta(n)\,\bigl[1-e^{-\tau_n}\bigr]\,dn ,\] and the cutoff, where the Rayleigh–Jeans black-body surface meets the optically-thin emission bound, scales as \[n^{*} \;\propto\; \Bigl(\tfrac{\omega_{pe}^{2}}{\omega_{ce}}\,a\Bigr)^{1/3} \;\sim\; a^{1/3},\] so the cutoff grows as the one-third power of the minor radius. Because a larger \(n^{*}\) moves more of the emission into the optically-thick (reabsorbed) band, the synchrotron loss fraction falls as the machine grows — the analytic origin of the length ladder below. At the design point \(n^{*}\approx24.7\), corrected for the finite mirror by a factor \((1+a/\ell)^{-1/3}\), and the reabsorbed loss sits far below the optically-thin estimate; the full electron-cyclotron transport calculation and its model spread are the subject of the companion synchrotron paper. Net electric output then follows the length ladder.

Length scaling and uncertainty

Net electric output scales with device length as the fixed end losses are amortized over a longer central cell: the generator delivers +104/+850/+2832 at \(55/440/1400\) m length (Fig. , gate KX-L1-A9). An uncertainty quantification over the design ranges (40 000 Monte-Carlo samples) returns a probability \(P(Q_E>1)=1.0\) with the central-cell beta the dominant driver (Fig. , gate KX-L1-A5-BU): the design closes robustly across its input ranges, conditional on the plug gate.

Direct energy conversion

Direct energy conversion recovers charged-particle power directly and is a bounded upside lever, not the closure mechanism. We take the converter module efficiency as a load-bearing input (\(\eta_{\mathrm{dec}}=0.7\)) and show through Eq. that the plant energy multiplication is gated by the recirculating plug and thermal-barrier power, not by conversion efficiency. A physics finding relocates the conversion problem: the dominant axial loss channel is a directed electron stream rather than the often-assumed directed-ion stream, which no published converter recovers — the subject of a companion paper proposing a quasineutral two-species expander converter.

Closure

The generator closes on the frozen physics model: \(Q_E=1.318\) and the high-field plug coil qualifies structurally within demonstrated reinforcement practice (a companion magnet paper retires the coil structural gate), leaving one residual physics gate. We use two-tier language: closed-on-model is what the frozen physics and buildable hardware deliver, and closed-demonstrated is the WHAM-scale plug-potential experiment (\(\sim\)2028–30) that retires the residual gate. Always naming the tier is part of the claim.

Discussion

The generator's neutron-source performance is robust and plug-independent: the 5.44 % neutron fraction and 233.95 MW neutron power stand regardless of the plug gate. Its net-power performance — \(Q_E=1.318\), +104/+850/+2832 over the length ladder — is conditional on retiring the plug-potential requirement, the one residual physics gate now that the coil structural gate is closed. Naming which results are robust and which are conditional is the honest way to present an advanced-fuel mirror.

Limitations

Every net-power number is contingent on retiring the plug-potential gate, which a design study cannot do. The converter efficiency is a declared input, not a computed result. The plug microstability is marginal, on the threshold rather than in a margin. The helium-3 fuel supply is a separate, lunar-gated problem. Each is stated with its evidence class.

Conclusion

The deuterium–helium-3 tandem-mirror generator reaches \(Q_E=1.318\) at 4298.5 MW with a neutron fraction of 5.44 %, delivering +104/+850/+2832 across a length ladder with \(P(Q_E>1)=1.0\). The binding gate is the plug-potential requirement \(n_p/n_c=16\), retired only by experiment; the coil structural gate is closed; the neutron-source performance is robust and the net-power performance conditional. The design is presented with that separation carried openly.
