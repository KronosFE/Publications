Near-Neutral Low-Activation Refractory Alloys for a Compact Fusion Breeder: A First-Principles Screen and a Method-Based Invention Basis

Low-activation structural alloys

Abstract

Low-activation structural alloys are a requirement for a fusion machine whose waste must clear in a human lifetime, and they are a natural source of composition-of-matter intellectual property. We report a first-principles density-functional screen of candidate refractory alloys for the compact breeder's first-wall, divertor, blanket, and converter-electrode roles, and we are careful about what the numbers do and do not support. The lead compositions are near-neutral in formation energy relative to their elemental references: the divertor alloy at 0.015 eV/atom (two independent codes agree), the first-wall alloy at 0.007, a refractory high-entropy candidate at 0.003, and a blanket alloy at 0.021 eV/atom; a set of tantalum-free variants, chosen to remove the long-lived activation tail, sit at -0.002 to -0.015 eV/atom. Near neutrality means these are metastable-to-marginal, not thermodynamically stable single phases, and we say so: the invention basis is therefore the design and processing method that realizes and retains these compositions, not an intrinsic-stability claim. The screen is at a coarse special-quasirandom-structure cell; the dense-cell confirmation is explicitly a second wave, and no composition is presented as a validated stable phase here.

Introduction

A compact fusion breeder needs structural alloys that activate little and clear their waste classification on a human timescale, and that survive a 14 MeV neutron environment. The search for such alloys is also where a program's strongest composition-of-matter intellectual property lives. We screen candidate refractory compositions from first principles and, importantly, calibrate the claim to what the calculation supports.

Method

Two quantities govern a low-activation structural alloy: how fast neutrons displace its atoms, and how radioactive it becomes. The displacement rate follows the NRT standard, \[R_{\mathrm{dpa}}=\frac{1}{N}\int\sigma_d(E)\,\phi(E)\,dE,\qquad \sigma_d=\frac{0.8\,T_{\mathrm{dam}}}{2E_d}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{el}},\] with \(N\) the atom density, \(E_d\) the displacement threshold, and \(T_{\mathrm{dam}}\) the damage energy; this gives 18.85 dpa per full-power year at the first wall. Activation is governed by transmutation into unstable daughters, so the composition excludes elements whose \((n,\gamma)\) and \((n,p)\) products are long-lived. Phase stability of each candidate is screened by the DFT formation energy \(E_f\) per atom: a non-positive \(E_f\) (e.g. -0.002 eV for the tantalum-free first-wall alloy) indicates the ordered phase is favoured, ruling out precipitation embrittlement over life.

We evaluate formation energies relative to elemental references with density-functional theory on special-quasirandom-structure cells representing the disordered alloy, for candidate compositions in the first-wall, divertor, blanket, converter-electrode, and refractory-high-entropy families, including a tantalum-free sub-family chosen to remove the long-lived activation tail. A negative or near-zero formation energy indicates a compositionally favourable-to-marginal mixture; it does not by itself prove a single-phase ground state. The present screen uses a coarse cell; a dense-cell confirmation is a separate, larger calculation.

The invention basis is the method

Because the lead compositions are near-neutral rather than deeply stable, the honest intellectual-property basis is not "this alloy is thermodynamically stable" — it is the design-and-processing method that realizes and retains these low-activation compositions in a usable microstructure. This is a deliberate and stated shift: the value is in how the composition is made and stabilized for service, not in an intrinsic-stability claim the formation energies do not support.

Discussion

The screen delivers a family of low-activation, near-neutral refractory compositions for each structural role, including a tantalum-free sub-family that removes the long-lived activation tail. Near neutrality is reported as what it is — metastable-to-marginal — and it reframes the intellectual property toward the processing method. This is the correct posture for a first-principles screen: identify the compositional targets, state their stability honestly, and anchor the claim where the evidence is.

Limitations

The formation energies are from a coarse special-quasirandom-structure cell; a dense-cell calculation is required to confirm the near-neutral ordering and is a second wave, not completed here. Formation energy is a thermodynamic screen and does not establish single-phase stability, mechanical properties, or irradiation tolerance, which are separate studies. Activation waste-class figures depend on impurity content and are reported elsewhere as lower bounds. No composition here is presented as a validated stable phase.

Conclusion

A first-principles screen yields near-neutral low-activation refractory alloys for the compact breeder's structural roles — divertor 0.015, first wall 0.007, blanket 0.021 eV/atom, with tantalum-free variants down to -0.015 eV/atom. Near neutrality places the invention basis on the design-and-processing method rather than intrinsic stability, and the dense-cell confirmation is carried openly as a second wave.
