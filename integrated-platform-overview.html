The Kronos Integrated Platform: A Spherical-Tokamak Breeder and a Deuterium-Helium-3 Burner as One Strategic-Materials and Power Architecture

The integrated two-machine platform

Abstract

We present the integrated Kronos platform as one architecture: a compact negative-triangularity spherical- tokamak breeder (Hyperion) that supplies strategic materials — tritium at up to 2.0 kg-T yr\(^-1\) per unit, helium-3, 14 MeV neutrons, and co-produced isotopes — and a deuterium–helium-3 burner in two housings (Aegis, MetroVolt) that supplies power at \(Q_E=1.318\) with a neutron fraction of only 5.44 %. The two machines share a REBCO magnet basis and a single physics de-risking recordR0, and they close a strategic loop: the breeder's helium-3 is a co-product, while the burner's bulk helium-3 fuel is lunar-gated around 2038–40. We state closure in two tiers throughout — closed-on-model (what the frozen physics delivers) versus closed-demonstrated (the named experiment that retires each residual gate) — and never say "closed" unqualified. The breeder closes-on-model as a platform, with demonstrated closure the measured tritium breeding ratio at first-of-a-kind operation; the burner closes-on-model at \(Q_E=1.318\) with a buildable coil, its one residual gate the plug potential, retired by a plug-scale experiment. The strong, defensible headline is that all three products close on the frozen physics, every component is buildable within demonstrated practice, and the distance to demonstrated closure is three named experiments, not open physics.

Introduction

Kronos is not one machine but a platform (Fig. ): a breeder that makes strategic materials and a burner that makes power, sharing a magnet basis, a control architecture, and a single frozen physics record. This paper is the integrated overview; each subsystem has a companion paper, and every headline number is a frozen anchor in the de-risking registerR0.

The breeder: a strategic-materials platform

The compact negative-triangularity spherical tokamak reaches \(Q=3.0763\) at 85.04 MW and supplies four products: tritium along a confirmed breeding ladder to 2.0 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) per unit (net TBR ceiling 1.42); helium-3 at about 0.9 kg-T yr\(^{-1}\) per unit; 14 MeV neutrons for materials qualification; and co-produced medical and industrial isotopes. Negative triangularity suppresses turbulence at both scales into a quiet subcritical core, the equilibrium is ideal-MHD-stable, and the inboard centrepost is a consumable, scheduled-replacement cartridge. Net electricity is a category error for the breeder and is excluded (companion breeder papers).

The burner: power in two housings

The deuterium–helium-3 tandem-mirror burner reaches \(Q_E=1.318\) at a neutron fraction of only 5.44 %, delivering +104/+850/+2832 across a device-length ladder. It is deployed in two housings — Aegis (fixed defence installations) and MetroVolt (data-centre generation) — that share the mirror physics and the direct-energy-conversion train. Its one residual physics gate is the plug potential (\(n_p/n_c=16\)); the high-field plug coil qualifies structurally, so the magnet is not the gate (companion burner and magnet papers).

The strategic helium-3 loop

Helium-3 links the two machines but does not close between them: the \(12.3\)-year tritium half-life makes the breeder's decay-sourced helium-3 far too slow to fuel a burner fleet (192/105/45 breeders per burner), so burner-scale helium-3 is lunar-gated around 2038–40. The breeder's helium-3 is a strategic co-product with national-security, cryogenic, and quantum value — distinct from bulk fuel supply, and distinct from revenue.

Program timeline

The build canon (Fig. ) is founder-locked: construction begins Q2 2027, first-of-a-kind tritium follows in 2030–31, the \(\sim\)100 MW test burner in 2032, and lunar helium-3 gates the burner fleet around 2038–40.

Closure, in two tiers

We state closure in two tiers throughout and never say "closed" unqualified (Table ). The breeder closes-on-model as a platform, demonstrated by the measured breeding ratio at first-of-a-kind operation. The burner closes-on-model at \(Q_E=1.318\) with a buildable coil, demonstrated by the plug-scale potential experiment. The defensible headline is that all three products close on the frozen physics, every component is buildable within demonstrated practice, and the distance to demonstrated closure is three named experiments, not open physics.

Discussion and conclusion

The value of the platform framing is that the two machines share more than a brand: a magnet basis, a control architecture, a de-risking record, and a strategic materials-to-power logic. The breeder makes the materials that a fusion economy needs — tritium, helium-3, neutrons, isotopes — while the burner makes power at low neutron output. Each closes on the frozen physics; each has one or a few named experiments between on-model and demonstrated; and none waits on open physics. That is the honest, defensible statement of where the platform stands.
